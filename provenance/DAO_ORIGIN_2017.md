## 3. The four-phase growth arc (sourced to ledger structure)

The claim "a personal experiment that became a social experiment that kept
growing" is observable in the **shape** of the ledger, not just its content.

### Phase 1 — personal experiment (2017)
One contributor. One row. No collaborators, no ticket queue, no failure state.
A personal experiment by definition.

### Phase 2 — personal experiment *with advisers* (2018–2020)
New names enter (Johnson Teh, Allison Liu, Ilya Voytov, Richard Chu, Vibhu
Mittal, Ryan Feathermen, Rob Hutter), but in a fixed pairing: they contribute
**insight**, Gary **implements** — e.g. *"Contributed key insight on market
 dispersion theory…"* paired with *"Implemented market sell off index…"*.
Social *input*; personal *execution*. The machine still runs on one pair of
hands.

### Phase 3 — the social experiment proper (2021)
Four structural markers appear together, none of which a solo project needs:

1. **Role specialization** — names arrive with *functions*, not just insights:
   Angeline Tan (copywriting), Val Lapidus (onboarding campaigns, brokerage
   integrations), Momo Yamada / Priscilla Huang (UX/design), Vishvajit (mobile
   API), Yousra Saad Kamoona & Val Lapidus (iOS / Android).
2. **A work queue** — `PDT-xx` ticket IDs. Triage implies more work than one
   actor can sequence.
3. **Outsiders** — `Street Insights LLC`, `Bala Vishal` (GTM playbooks);
   contracted capacity, not friends.
4. **The ledger learns to price failure** — `Failed / Partial shares issued`
   (Angeline Tan ×2, PDT-64 Robinhood, crypto sell-off index) and `Aborted`.
   **A personal experiment never needs a failure state.** The moment the ledger
   can say *"you tried, it failed, here is partial credit,"* it has become an
   institution that must be fair to people who are not its founder.

That fourth marker is the true hinge — the point where the system had to become
**fair** rather than merely **productive**.

### Phase 4 — practice as operating principle (now)
The mission itself rotates (market-observation → Agroverse → TrueSight DAO),
and the contemplative practice stops being adjacent to the build and becomes
its *operating principle*. Gary's framing: the later personal contributions are
*"what happens if I were to live the practice as part of daily life, as opposed
 to only as a side track from my main daily life."* (Testimony, not ledger.)

## 4. What remains unverified — do not overclaim

- **Interior state.** The ledger records *what* was contributed, never the
  state of mind it came from. "Practice lived as daily life" is Gary's account;
  the sheet cannot corroborate interiority.
- **Contributors ≠ devotees.** Many 2021 names may be paid contractors doing a
  job, not people who adopted a practice. The ledger proves **coordination
grew**; it does not prove **a community formed**. Those are different claims.
- **"First line" is the top row.** The sheet is not strictly date-sorted (e.g.
  a 2021 row sits above a 2018 one), so "top row = earliest" is asserted for
  the *first* entry only; an undated or earlier row deeper down is not fully
  excluded.
- **Name origin.** Nothing in the context corpus states whether "TrueSight"
  was derived from *vipassanā* ("true / clear seeing"). `search_context` for
  "true seeing", "TrueSight name origin", and "North Fork" all return **zero
  hits**. Unconfirmed — do not assert.
