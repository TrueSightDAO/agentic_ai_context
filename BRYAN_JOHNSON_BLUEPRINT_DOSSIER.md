## 3. The cocoa product — the commercially decisive detail

**Blueprint sells a cocoa powder as part of its core protocol.** Not chocolate — powder. And Johnson has published his buying criteria, which is effectively a **free spec sheet for the entire category**:

> **MY COCOA BUYING CRITERIA**
> 1. 100% pure cocoa
> 2. Un-dutched (not processed with alkali)
> 3. Tested for heavy metals
> 4. High percentage of cocoa flavanols — Blueprint has **400+ mg per serving**
> — *Bryan Johnson, "Why You Should Eat Chocolate Every Day" (YouTube, Apr 2024, 2.37M views)*

**Specs as sold:** 100% pure, unsweetened, un-dutched, **7.5% flavonols**, 400+ mg flavanols/serving, 340 g = 60-day supply. Publishes a **Certificate of Analysis** publicly.

**His stated reasoning (his own words, via NMN.com):**

> *"When you go to the store, you have no idea what you're buying. It could be really dirty… We sourced the world's best cocoa. We test it before we buy it. We test it when it's in our warehouse, and then we share the results with you. This is how the world should function."*

**And from the Blueprint blog ("Cocoa Longevity Benefits", Sept 2026):**
- Dutching (alkalinisation) reduces flavonoid content by **~60%**
- Chocolate carries high sugar; cocoa carries heavy metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic) that you *can't fully avoid* — so measure and choose lower
- Powder has the highest flavanol concentration of any cocoa form
- *"Cocoa has been one of the toughest products for our supply chain. Most cocoa powder available today doesn't fit our standards. We tested 10+ cocoa suppliers to find the cleanest one with the highest flavanols."*

**Health claims he attaches to it:** cardiovascular support, cholesterol, neuroprotection, healthy inflammatory response, nitric-oxide bioavailability/vasodilation. Served as hot chocolate, in coffee/smoothies, or blended into Nutty Pudding. Advises consuming before early afternoon (natural caffeine).

## 4. The protocol (compressed)

- **Diet:** largely plant-based; previously ~1,977 kcal/day. Signature dish *Nutty Pudding*; *Super Veggie*. Whole foods, leafy greens, cruciferous, legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains, healthy fats.
- **Supplements:** ~100/day at peak (tablet/capsule/powder) — **reduced to ~30 by March 2026** per Wikipedia. 2026 changes: NMN/NR six days not seven; **rapamycin dropped (2024)**; added low-dose lithium and NDGA.
- **Also** prescription meds for his own diagnosed conditions (levothyroxine, Armour Thyroid) — *not* general-protocol items.
- **Dropped when data didn't hold:** plasma transfusions, growth hormone, rapamycin. **This is the most intellectually honest thing in the protocol and worth respecting.**
- **Therapies:** sauna, HBOT (he reports +2.6% telomere length and hsCRP below detectable), light therapy.
- **Measurement:** regular biomarker panels, organ-specific aging clocks, skin-age imaging, and — via Kernel — brain monitoring.
- **Women's protocol** exists, presented by CMO Kate Tolo, phase-adjusted by menstrual cycle.

**Self-reported results at 48** (label as claims): muscle composition 98th percentile · bone mineral density 99th · blood glucose better than 98% of 18–25-year-olds · skin age 39 · fertility 99th percentile.

## 5. Criticism and what NOT to copy

This is a genuinely polarizing figure and a realistic partner assessment has to say so:

- **NYT (Mar 2025)** — reporting on his use of **confidentiality agreements** to seek control over people around him.
- **Wired (Jul 2025)** — *"Bryan Johnson Is Going to Die."*
- **No independent verification** of outcome claims; the measurement apparatus is his own.
- **N=1 with a $2M/yr budget and 30 doctors** is not a transferable protocol. Even his own careful advice reduces to: whole foods, consistent habits, personalised tracking.
- **Extreme supplement stacking** is a consumer and regulatory liability, not a template — the WSJ (Mar 2026) covered supplement stacks as wellness status symbols and questioned safety.
- He is a **marketing machine first, a scientist second.** The product is belief, sold with real data aesthetics.
