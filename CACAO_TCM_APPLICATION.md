## 0. What this document is — and the one thing it does not do

`OSCAR_FARM_CHINA_ACTION_PLAN.md` established three findings, and all three stand:

1. Cocoa is absent from both 药食同源目录 and the 保健食品 permitted-ingredient list — its only lawful Chinese identity is **普通食品**.
2. Cocoa has **no 《中国药典》 entry**, so there is no TCM status to borrow and no laboratory can output one (§1.3).
3. Therefore **TCM cannot be a regulatory unlock and cannot be a health claim.**

This document does **not** reopen any of that.

What it addresses is a question the action plan left open: *if TCM cannot be a claim, is there any other door it can enter through?*

There is. TCM's therapeutic engine is not the label claim — it is **配伍 (pèi wǔ, formulation pairing)** combined with **炮制 (páo zhì, processing)**. Both are **recipe** activities, not **labelling** activities. A recipe is not a claim. Which means:

| | Status |
|---|---|
| TCM as **regulatory unlock** | **Dead.** Unchanged. Gates are GACC, GB 2762, GB 2763, GB 2761. |
| TCM as **health claim** on 普通食品 packaging | **Dead.** Unchanged. |
| TCM as **health claim** in 1:1 conversation | **Narrow, translational only** — see §8. |
| TCM as **formulation framework** — the recipe | **Live.** This document. |

> **The whole thesis in one line:** use TCM to decide *what goes in the bag*; use plain food facts to decide *what goes on the label*. Nothing crosses between those two layers.

**The action plan's §1.3 rule remains binding throughout this document:** 性味 language derived here is **tone and design vocabulary only — zero regulatory authority, zero scholarly authority, never on a pack, never cited as TCM doctrine.**

## 1. 性味 (xìng wèi — nature and flavour), derived by method

### 1.1 Why derivation is legitimate, and where the risk sits

TCM has a *derivation* method: flavour is read off the substance's actual character, and channel entry follows from flavour via **五味归经** (five-flavour channel attribution), which is textbook doctrine.

This is different from fabricating an identity:

| Approach | What it does | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Assert cocoa's 性味归经 as established fact | Invents doctrine for something with no 药典 entry | **Fabrication.** This is what Chinese health media does, and the aggregators themselves admit 原始文献出处需要进一步查证. |
| Derive it by standard method, label it as our derivation, keep it internal | Applies a known method transparently | **Permissible as an internal design brief** — but still not doctrine |

The risk in the plan's §1.3 ("any 性味 language would be our own invention, which is a marketing choice with regulatory risk attached") is neutralised **entirely** by keeping it off the pack. That is exactly what §8 enforces.

### 1.2 Flavour, read off the chemistry

| 味 | Attributable to | 五味 action |
|---|---|---|
| **苦 (bitter)** | theobromine — the defining taste | 泄、燥、坚阴 — drains, dries, preserves yin. **苦入心** — bitter enters the Heart |
| **涩 (astringent)** | flavanols / tannins (astringency = protein binding) | 收敛固涩 — astringes and consolidates |
| **甘 (sweet)** | sugars, amino acids | 补、缓、和 — tonifies, moderates, harmonises. **甘入脾** — sweet enters the Spleen |
| **芳香 (aromatic)** | roast volatiles | 化湿、醒脾、开窍 — transforms damp, wakes the Spleen, opens the orifices |

### 1.3 Nature and channel entry

**性: 温 (warm).** Bitter + aromatic + stimulant is the classic warm profile. Note the conflict with the folk attribution: Baidu Health says 性温, so on this point our derivation and the internet copy agree — which is not corroboration, only convergence.

**归经: 心、脾 (Heart, Spleen).** Derived from 苦入心 and 甘入脾 — textbook 五味归经.

**A correction, recorded because it will otherwise get reused:** an earlier draft claimed 肾 (Kidney) via diuresis. That was loose. 利尿 (diuresis) is 利水渗湿 — draining dampness — which belongs to Bladder / Triple Burner / Spleen territory, **not** channel entry to the Kidney. 归肾 normally requires a salty flavour or a direct Kidney-tonifying action. **肾 is dropped.** Interestingly the HK column claims 归心脾肾 — it reaches the same third channel by the same loose route, which is further evidence that folk attribution is copied rather than derived.

## 2. 炮制 — the bridge from TCM to the production line

**性味 is set by processing.** This is the most operationally useful idea in the document.

Raw vs roasted vs Dutched cocoa produce genuinely different thermal character: 炒 (roasting/stir-frying) generally **increases 温**, and alkalisation (Dutching) shifts it further. Which gives a clean statement:

> **The ferment/roast protocol *is* 炮制. It determines the 性味.**

You are therefore not *claiming* a nature — you are **choosing** one through process. Three consequences:

1. **The flavour ↔ flavanol trade-off gains a third axis.** Turn-level discussions had it as two competing objectives; 性味 positioning is a third, process-controlled one.
2. **Product differentiation can be process-led.** Same farm, same vintage, two roast profiles = two positions in the 证 matrix (§5) without inventing a second supply chain.
3. **It is a legitimate manufacturing brief** even though it is not a label. A roaster can be told "target a deeper, warmer profile" and understand exactly what to do.

**Honest limit:** I have not measured whether Oscar's ferment/roast range is wide enough to move 性味 meaningfully. That is an empirical question, and much cheaper to answer than to argue.

## 3. 功效 (gōng xiào — actions), each with its mechanism

Stated so that every line has a mechanism attached. Note that the mechanism is the *evidence*; the TCM term is the *translation*.

| 功效 | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **活血通脉** — invigorate blood, free the vessels | Flavanols → endothelial function (the EU-authorised territory) |
| **醒神开窍** — rouse the spirit, open the orifices | Theobromine / caffeine; bitter + aromatic |
| **利水渗湿** — drain water, seep out damp | Theobromine diuresis (mild) |
| **燥湿化湿** — dry and transform damp | Bitter dries; aromatic transforms |
| **补益脾胃** — tonify Spleen and Stomach | Sweet tonifies (weak — cocoa is not a tonic) |

**What is not on this list, deliberately:** nothing about 肾, nothing about 补血, nothing about 安神. Cocoa is a stimulant food, not a tonic, and over-claiming actions internally produces a recipe that doesn't match the ingredient.

## 4. 禁忌 (jìn jì — contraindications) — where credibility is won

TCM always carries contraindications. Including them is what separates a framework from marketing copy.

| 禁忌 | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **阴虚火旺** — yin deficiency with false fire: dry mouth, night sweats, insomnia | 温燥 aggravates it |
| **痰湿内盛** — excess phlegm-damp | **肥甘厚味 (rich, fatty, sweet foods) create 痰湿.** This is TCM's genuine critique of chocolate, and it is correct. |
| **失眠、心悸** — insomnia, palpitations | Methylxanthine load |
| **湿热** — damp-heat | 温 + 甘 feeds it |

> Saying *"cocoa is 肥甘厚味, unsuitable for a phlegm-damp constitution"* costs nothing commercially and buys enormous credibility with anyone who knows 中医. It is also the single fastest way to distinguish our material from a brand reciting health-media copy.

This table is not decoration — it directly generates the 配伍 answer in §6.

## 5. 证 (zhèng — pattern) → product

TCM pairs a substance with a **pattern**, not a disease. This is the matrix that turns the framework into SKUs.

| 证 / consumer state | Product direction | Process logic |
|---|---|---|
| **心血瘀阻** — Heart blood stasis: cold extremities, dull complexion, chest oppression | High-flavanol cocoa powder | Medium/light roast; retain flavanols |
| **神疲乏力、清阳不升** — fatigue, sluggishness, clear yang failing to rise | Raw / light-roast cocoa | Higher theobromine, not drying |
| **寒湿、需温通** — cold-damp, needs warming and freeing | Dark roast + 配伍 (§6) | Warm + aromatic, transforms damp |
| **痰湿** — phlegm-damp | **DO NOT TARGET — contraindicated (§4)** | — |

**The last row is a real business decision, not a disclaimer.** The phlegm-damp segment is precisely where conventional chocolate lives, and TCM says don't go there. Our differentiation is the opposite segment: the person who wants the vascular and alerting effect without the 肥甘厚味 problem — which is, conveniently, the person who buys unsweetened cocoa powder rather than a bar.

## 6. 配伍 — the unlock, and the reason this is a product architecture

### 6.1 The structural argument

**Cocoa is not 药食同源. But every classic cocoa partner is. And 普通食品 may legally contain 药食同源 ingredients — that is precisely what the 106-item list exists for.**

So the TCM application is:

> **Use TCM 配伍 logic to formulate cacao with 药食同源 partners — products whose *design rationale* is TCM but whose *regulatory status* is ordinary food.**

No claim is made. No registration is triggered. The 配伍 logic stays in the recipe.

### 6.2 The partner table

**Membership verified:** Envoy independently confirmed all six are on the official 106-item 药食同源 list — 生姜 and 肉桂 from the original 2002 batch, the others in later additions.

| 配伍 partner | TCM rationale | Product form |
|---|---|---|
| **生姜 ginger** | 温中散寒 — warms the middle, disperses cold; moderates bitterness, strengthens the warming-freeing action | 固体饮料 |
| **肉桂 cinnamon** | 温通、引火归元、芳香 — warms and frees, draws fire back to source | 固体饮料 |
| **陈皮 aged tangerine peel** | **理气化湿 — directly counters 肥甘厚味生痰湿** | 代用茶 / 固体饮料 |
| **山楂 hawthorn** | 消食化积、活血 — resolves food stagnation, invigorates blood | 固体饮料 |
| **大枣 red date** | 补脾养血、甘缓 — tonifies Spleen, nourishes blood, moderates | 代用茶 |
| **枸杞子 goji** | 补肝肾 — tonifies Liver and Kidney | 固体饮料 |

**The 陈皮 row is the important one.** It answers the contraindication in §4: cacao is 肥甘厚味 → creates 痰湿; 陈皮 理气化湿 → resolves it. That is 配伍 doing its actual job, and it produces a product defensible to a TCM practitioner without a single claim on the label. Anyone who knows TCM reads the combination instantly.

### 6.3 Two cautions on this table

**(a) On the list ≠ cleared for this use.** These are two different claims and the difference is the whole compliance question:

- *"生姜 is on the 106-item list"* — **verified**
- *"生姜 at this dose in this product is permitted"* — **NOT checked**

Each partner has a **使用量 cap** and often a use-form restriction. Known examples of the shape: 人参 ≤3g/day; 当归 spice-use only ≤3g/day. **None of the six above has had its cap checked yet.** This is not blocking for the design brief; it *is* blocking before formulation.

**(b) Label naming must use the official list term.** Colloquial and list names differ — 大枣 not 红枣, 枸杞子 not 枸杞, and the peel's official term needs checking against 陈皮/橘皮. A label must print the name the regulation uses.

## 7. China product definition — which form, against which standard

What we can lawfully call the product is decided by standards, not by us.

| Form | Chinese standard | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **可可粉 (cocoa powder)** | **GB/T 20706 可可粉** | ★ Has its own national standard |
| 可可液块 (liquor) | **GB/T 20705 可可液块及可可饼块** | Named raw material |
| 可可脂 (butter) | GB/T 20707 | Named raw material |
| 巧克力 (chocolate) | **GB/T 19343-2016** (definition/classification) + **GB 9678.2** (safety) | Finished product |
| 可可豆 (beans) | Imported agricultural commodity — needs GACC registration | B2B only |
| **可可碎 (nibs)** | **No dedicated national standard** | ⚠️ Category genuinely unclear |

Also binding on any pack: **GB 7718** (prepackaged food labelling) and **GB 28050** (nutrition labelling).

**Recommendation: 可可粉 as the hero China SKU.**

- It has its own product standard — no argument about category
- Drinkable, matching the 茶-adjacent usage habit the TCM framing also wants
- Avoids 巧克力's compositional rules on sugar and fat entirely
- The pleasing irony: **powder is the most cadmium-exposed form in the EU but the least gated form in China** — favourable on both axes

**For 配伍 SKUs, 固体饮料 (solid beverage) is the cleaner route** over 代用茶 for a cocoa base; both formats carry their own standards.

## 8. The three registers — and the line that keeps this lawful

This is the operational core of the document. One piece of knowledge, four destinations.

| Layer | Register | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Design brief** — internal, to Peter / the formulator / Oscar's sister | **Full TCM** | 性味、归经、功效、证型、配伍、禁忌. Use all of it. |
| **Label and detail page** | **Product facts only** | Ingredients, origin, per-bag QR, assay results, net weight, standard compliance |
| **1:1 conversation** with someone who thinks in TCM | **Translation, invitational** | *"从中医角度看，可可的作用落在心主血脉这一功能范畴"* — then stop and let them complete it |
| **Never, anywhere public** | — | 功效 verbs, 归经, 证 names, 性味 as fact, anything with 治疗 / 改善 / 缓解 |

### 8.1 Why the 1:1 register is acceptable and the pack register is not

**On a pack, an unsourced 性味 statement is an unsubstantiated representation — regulator bait.**

**In conversation, naming a real TCM functional system is a translation act.** 心主血脉 (*the Heart governs the blood and the vessels*) is genuine doctrine. We are not claiming anything about cocoa's status; we are saying *the system this acts on is one TCM already names*. And we attach a lawful basis that is not TCM at all — the EU-authorised wording.

**The strongest form of this is to hand the conclusion to the practitioner.** Lead with the mechanism and the EU claim; let a 中医-credentialled person make the TCM connection themselves. It lands harder and it becomes their insight rather than our claim.

### 8.2 The on-pack safe register

> **巴西巴伊亚 · 单一庄园可可**
> 一袋一码 · 全项检测
>
> - 产自巴西巴伊亚州单一庄园
> - 每袋二维码可追溯至具体批次
> - 未经碱化（非荷兰工艺），不添加糖
> - 黄曲霉毒素、重金属、微生物 — 全项检测
> - 可可黄烷醇含量经第三方实验室检测

Every line is a **verifiable fact**. Note the last one: *"flavanol content measured by third-party laboratory"* is a **content** statement, not an **effect** statement. That is the line you cannot cross, and we don't need to.

The one TCM-adjacent line that **is** safe on pack: **可可自古为饮，属食养之品，不作药用** — *cocoa has traditionally been a drink, a nourishing food, not a medicine.* True, modest, and it does real work: it says we are not pretending to be a drug, which is what an informed Chinese buyer wants to hear from a foreign brand.

### 8.3 The never-say list

| ❌ | Why |
|---|---|
| 归心、脾、肾经 | Unsourced; the aggregators themselves say 原始文献出处需要进一步查证 |
| 可可碱扩张血管、保护心脏 | Drug-axis language → 食品/药品 boundary risk |
| 治疗 / 改善 / 缓解 + condition | 功效 claim on 普通食品 |
| 药食同源 (of cocoa) | Factually false — not on the 106-item list |
| Port the EFSA wording to China | No legal standing there; invites scrutiny |

## 12. One-paragraph summary

The action plan is right that TCM is not a regulatory unlock and cannot be a claim in China; nothing here changes that. But TCM's therapeutic engine was never the claim — it is 配伍 and 炮制, which are recipe activities, and a recipe is lawful. Cocoa is not 药食同源, but its classic partners are, and ordinary food may legally contain them, so TCM can drive the *formulation* while the *label* carries only verifiable facts — origin, per-bag QR lineage, assay results, standard compliance. The strongest single product move in the whole framework is 可可 + 陈皮, because it answers TCM's own genuine objection to chocolate (肥甘厚味 → 痰湿) using 配伍 rather than denial. And the honest status is that the 药食同源 architecture is now verified, while the 性味/归经 derivation and the 证→product matrix remain synthesis requiring a practitioner's eyes before anything ships — with per-partner usage caps still unchecked, because *"on the list"* and *"cleared for this use"* are two different claims. The first SKU needs none of the unresolved items: single-estate cocoa powder, standard roast, provenance and assay on the pack, TCM only in the design brief. Ship that while the rest is vetted.
