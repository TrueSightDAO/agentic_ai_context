## 3. Functional-health evidence base for cocoa

Assembled separately because **the CIC does not publish in this lane** — its output is agronomy, genetics, fermentation, and sensory. Any health claim we make must be anchored in the literature below, not in CIC work.

### 3a. Vascular / blood pressure — the strongest signal

| Year | Title | Venue | Cites | DOI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | Impact of cocoa flavanols on blood pressure and vascular function: updated systematic review & meta-analysis of RCTs | J. Nutr. Health Aging | 0 | `10.1016/j.jnha.2026.100966` |
| 2014 | Cocoa flavanol consumption improves cognitive function, BP control, and metabolic profile in elderly subjects — **CoCoA Study** (RCT) | Am. J. Clin. Nutr. | **344** | `10.3945/ajcn.114.092189` |
| 2012 | Benefits in Cognitive Function, Blood Pressure, and Insulin Resistance Through Cocoa Flavanol Consumption in Elderly With Mild Cognitive Impairment | Hypertension | **311** | `10.1161/hypertensionaha.112.193060` |
| 2010 | Cocoa flavanols: effects on vascular nitric oxide and blood pressure | J. Clin. Biochem. Nutr. | 101 | `10.3164/jcbn.11-010fr` |
| 2010 | Dose-related effects of flavanol-rich cocoa on blood pressure | J. Human Hypertension | 80 | `10.1038/jhh.2009.105` |
| 2021 | Blood-pressure-lowering effects of cocoa flavanols in diabetes mellitus: systematic review & meta-analysis | J. Functional Foods | 14 | `10.1016/j.jff.2021.104399` |

**Mechanism:** flavanols increase nitric-oxide bioavailability → endothelium-dependent vasodilation.

### 3a-bis. The authorised health claim — the most valuable asset in this category

There is exactly one **legally authorised** cocoa-flavanol claim in the EU, and it is unusually usable. Commission Regulation (EU) No 851/2013 (amending 432/2012), Art. 13(5), granted on Barry Callebaut proprietary data:

> **"Cocoa flavanols help maintain the elasticity of blood vessels, which contributes to normal blood flow."**

Conditions of use, verbatim from the EU health-claims register:
- Consumer must be informed the benefit is obtained with **200 mg cocoa flavanols/day**
- Usable only for **cocoa beverages (with cocoa powder)** or **dark chocolate** providing ≥200 mg/day
- The flavanols must be **degree of polymerisation 1–10**
- Also permitted for capsules/tablets of high-flavanol cocoa extract

EFSA's equivalence (2014 opinion, `EFSA Journal 2014;12(5):3654`): 200 mg cocoa flavanols ≈ **2.5 g high-flavanol cocoa powder**, ~10 g high-flavanol dark chocolate, or 0.25–0.67 g cocoa extract.

**Why it matters commercially:** it names *cocoa powder* explicitly, needs only ~2.5 g per serving, and requires 200 mg of an actually-measurable quantity. It is the only claim in this space that a lawyer will defend.

**Do not conflate this with the rejected claims.** EFSA's 2010 opinion (`10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1792`) declined the **blood-pressure** and **lipid-oxidation** wordings. The authorised claim concerns vasodilation / blood flow only. Any blood-pressure claim remains unauthorised in the EU despite strong trial evidence.

### 3b. Cognition — mixed, and this matters commercially

The **COSMOS** trial (Brigham & Women's / Fred Hutch / Columbia, `cosmostrial.org`) is the decisive large-scale test:

- 21,442 participants; 12,666 women ≥65, 8,776 men ≥60; median 3.6 y follow-up
- Intervention: cocoa extract with **500 mg/d cocoa flavanols incl. ~80 mg (−)-epicatechin**

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Total CVD events (primary) | **Not significant** |
| CV death | **27% reduction** — described as *suggestive*, stronger with regular adherence |
| Cognition overall (COSMOS-Mind) | **Cocoa extract did NOT affect cognition.** The *multivitamin* did |
| Cognition, low-baseline-flavanol subgroup (COSMOS-Web) | Benefit seen in those with lower dietary flavanol intake |

Supporting papers:
- Sesso et al., *Effect of cocoa flavanol supplementation for prevention of CVD events: COSMOS RCT*, Am J Clin Nutr 2022;115(6):1490-1500. `10.1093/ajcn/nqac055`
- Baker et al., *Effects of cocoa extract and a multivitamin on cognitive function: a randomized clinical trial*, Alzheimers Dement 2023;19(4):1308-1319. `10.1002/alz.12767`
- Brickman et al., PNAS 2023 — dietary flavanols and hippocampal-dependent memory. `10.1073/pnas.2216932120`

**The single most important quote for us**, from the COSMOS investigators' own FAQ:

> *"…variation in processing of the cocoa bean and its negative impact on flavanol content makes chocolate an unreliable source of flavanols. Chocolate products do not typically indicate their flavanol content."*

That sentence explains the entire commercial structure of this category (see `CACAO_IN_QUANTIFIED_BIOHACKING.md`).

Brazil's Cd profile — which is what governs our own export exposure to EU limits — is **not well covered by an obvious searchable corpus** and needs a proper literature search plus direct lab data. **Do not assume Brazil is low-cadmium.**

## 4. How to refresh this bibliography

```bash
# CIC-affiliated papers (the core set)
curl 'https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=raw_affiliation_strings.search:%22Centro%20de%20Inova%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20do%20Cacau%22&per-page=50&select=doi,title,publication_year,cited_by_count'

# UESC institution roll-up (15,743 works, 266,997 citations)
curl 'https://api.openalex.org/institutions?search=Universidade%20Estadual%20de%20Santa%20Cruz'

# A cacao/CIC researcher's full output — swap the ORCID
curl 'https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=author.orcid:0000-0001-8690-7372'
```

**Watch-outs:** OpenAlex affiliation strings are author-entered and inconsistent ("Centro de Inovação do Cacau, Universidade Estadual de Santa Cruz, Ilhéus" vs bare "UESC"). The Micronutrients paper (§2b) proves the point — CIC output exists that an affiliation filter misses. Cross-check against `pctsul.org/blog/cic` announcements and the CIC Instagram before declaring the list complete.
