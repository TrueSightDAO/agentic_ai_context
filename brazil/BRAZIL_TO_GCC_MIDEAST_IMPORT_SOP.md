# Brazil → GCC / Middle East Cacao Import — End-to-End SOP

> **Scope:** Qatar · United Arab Emirates (Dubai) · Saudi Arabia — the **GCC / Middle East** lane for raw Brazilian cacao beans.
> **Purpose:** Single reference for AI assistants and human operators managing the export of raw cacao beans from Brazil into the Gulf Cooperation Council markets — market-access requirements, registration platforms, Certificates of Conformity (CoC), labelling, costs and processing times — so the lane can be evaluated in parallel with the Mainland-China (GACC-blocked) and Hong Kong lanes.
> **Prepared:** 2026-09-17 | **Prepared by:** Sophia Truesight (TrueSight DAO Autopilot)
> **Sources:** Governor-supplied 2-page guide *"Market Access Requirements and Operational Guidelines for Raw Cacao Bean Exports to Qatar, UAE (Dubai) and Saudi Arabia"* + governor-supplied processing-times/validity-periods table (image). Figures cross-checked against **Intertek / SGS (Qatar CoC)**, **USDA FAS GAIN (UAE food import)**, **SFDA / Cotecna / trade.gov (Saudi)**, and the **GCC Unified Customs Tariff**. Per-market figures are **as verified** at prep time — re-confirm at booking (see §10).

---

## 1. Why this lane — strategic framing

- The Brazil-side exporter (Black King / Matheus) holds a **MAPA** number but **GACC (Mainland China) registration is not obtained** — the Mainland lane is blocked.
- The **Middle East (GCC)** is a **parallel path worth evaluating**: it does **not** require GACC (GCC ≠ China) and the import threshold is materially lower than the Mainland's.
- **Hong Kong** is a **third, separate** lane (see `BRAZIL_TO_HONG_KONG_IMPORT_SOP.md`).
- ⚠️ **GCC ≠ one market.** The three target countries share the GCC/GSO *baseline* but each runs its **own** registration platform, CoC regime, labelling nuance and VAT rate. Do not treat a Qatar clearance as evidence for Saudi.

---

## 2. Baseline requirements — GCC unified framework

All three target states are GCC members and share a foundational set of requirements based on **GSO** standards:

| Item | Baseline |
|---|---|
| **Technical standards** | Must comply with **GSO** food-safety standards. |
| **System certifications** | **ISO 22000**, **HACCP**, etc. are *typically* required. |
| **Labelling language** | All information must be in **Arabic**; **English permitted in parallel**. |
| **Base tariff** | **GCC common external tariff = 5%** of CIF value (most goods). |
| **Halal** | Not applicable to raw cacao *per se*, but relevant downstream for finished/processed SKUs and for any animal-derived inputs. |

---

## 3. Qatar — WATHEQ + CoC

- **Importer registration:** importers must register in the **Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) WATHEQ** system and obtain a **Product Registration Number (PRN / QFR)**. Both the **food product** and the **importer** must be registered in WATHEQ.
- **Certificate of Conformity (CoC):** **prior to arrival**, each consignment must obtain a **CoC** issued by an **approved Conformity Assessment Body** (e.g. **SGS, Intertek, TÜV**). Applications are typically **initiated in the country of supply** and require: commercial invoice, packing list, batch test reports, **ISO 17025** laboratory reports, and **label photos**.
  - *Why it matters:* consignments with a valid CoC from an approved CAB benefit from **fast-track clearance** and reduced destination inspection by Qatar Customs.
- **Tariffs & taxes:** standard food tariff **5%**; **no VAT** currently applied.

---

## 4. UAE (Dubai) — DED licence + FIRS (+ MOCCAE)

- **Trade licence:** the importer must hold a **trade licence issued by the DED** that **includes food-trading activities**.
- **FIRS registration + product permits:** companies must register in the **Dubai Municipality Food Import and Re-export System (FIRS)**; **each product (SKU) must be registered**, requiring **label designs, ingredient lists, and Certificates of Analysis (CoA)**. New-product registration typically requires **lab testing at the Dubai Municipality Central Laboratory**.
- **Plant products:** raw cacao beans are a **plant product** and **may require an import permit from MOCCAE** (Ministry of Climate Change and Environment).
- **Tariffs & taxes:** tariff typically **5%**, **VAT 5%**.
- ⚠️ **ECAS caveat (source-guide correction):** the source guide states "*certain food categories require an ECAS Certificate of Conformity*." **This is doubtful.** **ECAS** (Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme, run by **MoIAT**) applies to **non-food regulated products** (electrical, tyres, etc.). Food conformity in Dubai runs through **FIRS / Dubai Municipality**, not ECAS. Treat the guide's ECAS-for-food claim as a likely **ECAS/FIRS conflation — verify with the importer / Dubai Municipality before budgeting for it.**

---

## 5. Saudi Arabia — SFDA Ghad + FASEH CoC

- **SFDA registration:** importers must register on the **SFDA Ghad platform**, and **register the product** (product registration is generally *optional* but often required by buyers/retail).
- **Certificate of Conformity — ⚠️ CRITICAL, CHANGED 2026-08-30:** the **FASEH platform is now MANDATORY for ALL food-shipment CoCs**. Per the SFDA circular, **effective 30 August 2026** all food-shipment Certificates of Conformity **must be issued through FASEH**, and **certificates issued outside the platform are no longer accepted**. Importers submit shipment-clearance requests via FASEH.
  - The source guide's framing ("*as of April 1, 2026, certain Brazilian products — e.g. poultry — added to the mandatory CoC list; advisable to confirm the latest status for raw cacao*") is **superseded**: as of the 30 Aug 2026 mandate, this is **not** category-specific — **every food shipment needs a FASEH-issued CoC.**
- **Labelling:** labels must contain **Arabic**, and the **Arabic information must not be smaller than** the other language(s).
- **Shelf life:** upon arrival, **remaining shelf life must typically be ≥ ⅓ of total shelf life** (some categories require **¾**).
- **Tariffs & taxes:** tariff on **unroasted cacao beans ≈ 5%**; **VAT = 15%**, calculated on **CIF value + customs duty**.

## 6. Processing times & validity periods (governor-supplied)

| Country | Item | Processing time | Validity period | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Qatar** | Importer Registration (WATHEQ) | — | **3 years**, renewable | |
| **Qatar** | Certificate of Conformity (CoC) | **~11 days (7 expedited)** | **Single shipment**; product registration **1 year** | Plan **at least 2–4 weeks** in advance; **test reports must be issued within 1 year** |
| **UAE (Dubai)** | FIRS Product Registration | **5–7 working days** (**3–6 weeks** with lab testing) | **5 years** | Subject to ongoing regulatory compliance |
| **UAE (Dubai)** | ECAS Certificate of Conformity (CoC) | **~2–3 weeks** after complete docs | **1 year**, renewable | Must be submitted by a **locally authorised representative in the UAE** |
| **Saudi Arabia** | Importer Registration (Ghad Platform) | **~5 working days** | **5 years**, renewable | |
| **Saudi Arabia** | Product Registration (Ghad Platform) | **~5 working days** (may take weeks to 2 months) | **1–3 years** (optional) | |
| **Saudi Arabia** | Certificate of Conformity (FASEH Platform) | **PCoC: 2–3 working days**; **SCoC: 1–2 working days** | **PCoC: 1 year** (reusable); **SCoC: single shipment** | **CoC must be issued at least 5 days prior to cargo arrival** |

> *Source: governor-supplied reference table. Actual processing times may vary — re-check at booking.*

## 7. Brazil-side export prerequisites (shared — do not duplicate)

The Brazil-origin phases are **identical across the HK / GCC / SF lanes**: export **NF-e (model 55)**, RADAR, SISCOMEX / DU-E, the **Black King CNPJ blocker**, pallet compliance (ISPM#15 / IPPC), and *desembaraço* by Omega. See `BRAZIL_EXPORT_LANE_LEARNINGS.md` §2 and `BRAZIL_TO_SF_FREIGHT_PREFLIGHT_CHECKLIST.md` Phase 0/1. **No GACC is required for an HK- or GCC-bound shipment** (GACC is a Mainland-China requirement only).

## 8. Pre-flight checklist

### Phase 0 — Pre-flight gates (do these FIRST)
- [ ] **Verify importer qualifications** ⚠️ **#1 gate.** Confirm with the Middle Eastern buyer whether their company has completed registration **and activated an account** in the target system: **Qatar WATHEQ / Dubai FIRS / Saudi Ghad**.
- [ ] **Secure the CoC route.** Contact an approved CAB (**SGS / Intertek / TÜV**, or Saudi via **FASEH**) to complete testing and obtain the CoC **before shipment**. Arriving without a CoC → customs delays, fines, or **return of the goods**.
- [ ] **Secure Arabic labelling** in advance — mandatory. Send label designs to the buyer / local authority for **pre-approval** to avoid on-site rectification.
- [ ] **Confirm product/SKU registration** requirements per market (UAE FIRS = per-SKU mandatory; Saudi product reg often buyer-driven).
- [ ] **Confirm MOCCAE import permit** need for raw cacao beans (plant product) into the UAE.
- [ ] **Build the full cost model** — see §9 (Saudi VAT 15% dominates).
- [ ] **Confirm the Brazil-side export is unblocked** (NF-e issuable — Black King CNPJ status; learnings §2).

### Phase 1 — Brazil origin & export (shared)
- [ ] Cargo packed, weighed, photographed at origin (Matheus, Ilhéus).
- [ ] Pallet compliance (ISPM#15 + IPPC stamp) if pallets used.
- [ ] Export NF-e issued; DU-E / SISCOMEX cleared; *desembaraço* by Omega.

### Phase 2 — Freight (Brazil → GCC)
- [ ] Book air/sea freight; confirm Incoterms; **insure the cargo**.
- [ ] Share AWB/BL with the importer + CAB **ahead** of arrival.

### Phase 3 — Import & customs
- [ ] CoC issued (and, for Saudi, **via FASEH, ≥5 days before arrival**).
- [ ] Declaration lodged; retain invoice, packing list, BL/AWB, CoO, health cert, CoA.
- [ ] File the import record to the DAO ledger (see §10).

## 9. Cost model (tariffs + VAT)

| Market | Tariff (raw cacao) | VAT | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Qatar** | **5%** | **0%** (none currently) | CoC/testing costs are the main extras. |
| **UAE (Dubai)** | **5%** | **5%** | Plus FIRS registration + lab testing + (possible) MOCCAE permit. |
| **Saudi Arabia** | **~5%** | **15%** — on **CIF + duty** | Materially higher than Qatar/UAE — **factor into any quote.** |

## 10. DAO ledger / inventory side

A GCC import is a **custody transfer between known supply-chain holders** (not an end-customer retail sale):
- Record an **[INVENTORY MOVEMENT]** (manager → GCC recipient) — QR status stays **IN INVENTORY**. Do **not** use a SALES EVENT unless the bag is sold to an end customer.
- Reference the AWB/BL and the import declaration / CoC in the event notes.
- If SKUs are later sold in the GCC, record a **[SALES EVENT]** at that point (QR → SOLD).

## 12. Open questions / to verify before booking

1. **Is there an identified GCC buyer/importer, and are they registered** in the target system (WATHEQ / FIRS / Ghad)? *The source guide assumes a buyer exists — none is named in DAO context yet.*
2. **Is the CoC route (SGS/Intertek/TÜV, or FASEH for Saudi) already engaged?** Get quotes for testing + CoC.
3. **Confirm the UAE ECAS-vs-FIRS question** (§4) with the importer / Dubai Municipality.
4. **Confirm Saudi FASEH mandate applies** to this consignment and the exact submission lead time (≥5 days pre-arrival).
5. **Arabic label artwork** — who produces/approves it, and when?
6. **Freight quote Brazil → Doha / Dubai / Riyadh-Jeddah** — not yet obtained.
7. **Halal / downstream SKU** implications if beans are converted for GCC retail.
