## 0. Headline

> ### FDA Import Alert **34-01** — *"Detention Without Physical Examination of Cocoa Beans From
> Brazil, Indonesia and Malaysia Due to Presence of Live Insects"* (DWPE, published **03/05/2026**).

**No Brazilian firm appears on its Green List.** The alert's Green List names **2 firms, both
Indonesian**. Per the alert's own Guidance:

> *"Divisions may detain, without physical examination, **all shipments of cocoa beans from Brazil**,
> Indonesia, Malaysia, **which are not listed on the Green List** of this alert."*

⇒ **Every cocoa-bean shipment from Brazil is subject to DWPE by default.** Ours included. This was
**not** preventable by better paperwork, and it is **not** a documentation defect. It is a
country-wide enforcement posture that pre-exists this shipment.

## 1. The reframe that matters most — **which agency's hold is it?**

Our notice said **"agriculture hold."** That is **CBP / AQI** vocabulary — the Agricultural Quarantine
and Inspection program — which points at **APHIS plant health**.

**Import Alert 34-01 is FDA**, and would ordinarily surface as an **FDA hold**, not an agriculture hold.

| | CBP / AQI "agriculture hold" | FDA hold |
|---|---|---|
| Agency | CBP, on APHIS authority | FDA |
| Concern | plant pest / disease, wood packaging | adulteration, filth, FSVP |
| Vocab | USDA hold, AQI, emergency action notice | Notice of FDA Action, DWPE |
| Our cause if this | phyto / APHIS permit / ISPM#15 wood | **Import Alert 34-01** (live insects) |

**We do not know which one we have.** That is now the single highest-value question for the broker —
it determines which of the two remediation routes below is even applicable. Do **not** let the
reporting vocabulary decide it for us.

## 2. Why 34-01 is nonetheless the sharpest candidate

| Signal | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Charge** | *Section 801(a)(3)* — article *"appears to consist in whole or in part of a filthy, putrid, or decomposed substance"*. OASIS charge code **`FILTHY`**. This is an **adulteration** allegation, not a paperwork defect. |
| **Historical violation rate** | Baseline window: **44 of 57** Brazilian cocoa-bean shipments detained — **77%**. (Indonesia 96%, Malaysia 57%.) |
| **Test method** | **MPM V-18** — FDA's official macroanalytical cocoa-bean method. Objective and measurable, so "discretion" is bounded by a lab result. |
| **Our specific exposure** | Air-freighted, consolidated, and **held in storage** — all insect-pressure factors. Storage time is *itself* a risk factor. |

**Relevant sibling alerts** (same product family, all currently active):
- **23-14** — mycotoxins (published 06/22/2026)
- **99-19** — *Salmonella* (10/06/2026)
- **99-46** — filth (07/31/2026)
- **99-41** — **FSVP non-compliance** (10/02/2026) — *this one is squarely about our own Fsvp obligations*

## 4. The release route — documented, and purchasable

34-01 sets out exactly how to obtain release. Quoting:

> *"In order to secure release of an individual shipment detained pursuant to this Import Alert, the
> importer, owner, consignee and/or another responsible party to the shipment, should provide evidence
> which demonstrates that the product does not contain live insects at a level that is determined to be
> filthy… Such evidence could include private laboratory analysis of a representative sample from a lot
> of the detained product."*

**Sampling protocol (verbatim specifics):**
- **≥ 10 representative subsamples per lot**
- **1 lb of beans** per subsample, *"composited by taking approximately one third (1/3) from each of
  three (3) bags"* in the lot
- Analyse per **MPM V-4A / V-18**
- Submit to the **FDA Division compliance office** named on the **Notice of FDA Action**

⚠️ **FDA retains discretion:** *"following receipt and review of private laboratory results, the FDA
may, at its discretion, collect and analyze audit samples before rendering a final decision."*

## 5. Two things that are genuinely reassuring

1. **A hold is not a finding.** APHIS's own importer page: *"The hold may likely mean that CBP hasn't
   gotten to it yet, and it could be temporary."* An **emergency action notice** is issued *only* if
   CBP/APHIS **confirm** a problem during inspection. **We have not received one.** Absent an EAN, we
   are in a queue, not in a violation.
2. **The ASTRA fumigation service is more relevant than I credited.** 34-01 is about **live insects**.
   Warehouse dedetização (service code **0713** / CNAE **8122200**) is **directly on-point evidence**
   for an insect/filth release package. It is still **not** a phytosanitary certificate — but for *this*
   alert it is genuinely useful. I had filed it as irrelevant to the phyto question; that was right for
   phyto and wrong for 34-01. **Correction noted.**

## 7. Honest gaps — what I could NOT verify

1. **ACIR was not queryable.** `acir.aphis.usda.gov` requires **SAML/SSO** login (I got redirects and
   `401`s). APHIS additionally **withdrew the three commodity manuals from public aphis.usda.gov on
   03/10/2022**, keeping them on an internal CBP/PPQ site. ⇒ **The authoritative APHIS requirement
   lookup for cocoa beans from Brazil remains unverified by me.** It needs the broker, or a credentialed
   human. I will not guess at it.
2. **We do not have the hold notice itself** — no emergency action notice, no CBP message, no ACE entry
   status. Everything above is inference **from published rules**, not from our own entry record. That
   distinction should survive into any decision we make.
3. **Not on any Red List.** I searched for `Black King` and `Matheus Reis` — **34-01 has no Red List at
   all**, which is precisely why the Green List is the operative test.

## 8. Recommended next actions, in order

1. **Get the actual notice.** Ask the broker: *is this an AQI/USDA hold or an FDA hold?* Request any
   **emergency action notice**, CBP message, or ACE entry status. **Everything else is downstream of this.**
2. **Confirm which articles are implicated** — specifically whether the **15 kg of whole beans** is named.
3. **If it is 34-01:** commission the **≥10-subsample private lab panel** per the protocol in §4. Do not
   buy a fumigation certificate; buy *evidence*. Budget it properly — this is the documented release path.
4. **If it is AQI/APHIS:** the phyto / APHIS-permit / wood-packaging line reopens and §7.1 (ACIR) becomes
   mandatory before we spend anything.
5. **Note the storage clock** — held storage costs accrue while we wait, and the delay is at least partly
   shared. Worth raising, not yet worth arguing.
