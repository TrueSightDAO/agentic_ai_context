## 0. Two headline answers, up front

### Answer 1 — TCM is the wrong door, and the premise has a category error in it

Cocoa appears on **neither** of China's two gating lists. Verified against both:

| List | What it controls | Cocoa present? |
|---|---|---|
| **药食同源目录** (food-medicine homology, 106 items) | Whether a substance may be positioned as both food and medicine | ❌ **Absent** |
| **可用于保健食品的物品名单** (卫法监发51号 — health-food permitted ingredients) | The only ingredients a 保健食品 may contain | ❌ **Absent** |

So cacao's only lawful identity in China is **普通食品 (ordinary food)**. That means: no 保健食品 "blue-hat" claim, no 药食同源 positioning, no functional claim of any kind, absent a multi-year registration we have no reason to attempt yet.

**But the deeper issue is conceptual.** You asked for a lab test to be *translated* into TCM. That cannot be done, by anyone:

> **性味归经** (nature / flavour / meridian attribution) is assigned by **doctrine and Pharmacopoeia entry**, not derived from analytical chemistry. A laboratory measures a substance. It does not classify it. There is no institute — in China or anywhere — that performs "lab result → TCM category" as a service, because that is not a chemical determination.

So the useful version of the question is narrower and *is* answerable:

- **Does 《中国药典》 / 《中华本草》 / 《中药大辞典》 already contain a 可可 (cacao) entry** with a stated 性味归经?
  - **If yes** → an existing TCM vocabulary hook exists, and we can *use* it as cultural narrative (never as a health claim).
  - **If no** → there is no TCM status to borrow. Any 性味 language would be our own invention, which is a marketing choice with regulatory risk attached.

This is **Task A1** below. It is free to answer and it settles the whole TCM question.

### Answer 2 — the real gates are three, and all three are bureaucratic, not scientific

| # | Gate | Requirement | Cocoa's exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **GACC Decree 248** (effective 2022-01-01) | Every overseas food **manufacturer, processor and storage facility** exporting to China must register with the General Administration of Customs. The **China Registration Number must be printed on both inner and outer packaging**. Registration is only valid for the specific HS + quarantine-code combination registered. | Cocoa is not in GACC's Group 1 (the 18 categories needing home-authority recommendation, e.g. meat/dairy/aquatic); it most likely falls in **Group 2 — self-application**. **Verify before assuming.** |
| **2** | **GB 2762** 食品中污染物限量 | For **可可制品、巧克力和巧克力制品以及糖果**: **lead 0.5 mg/kg (as Pb)**, **arsenic — no limit**, **cadmium — no limit**. Verified against both the 2022 text and the 2025 revision. | **RESOLVED — China sets no cadmium limit for cocoa.** See §3. |
| **3** | **GB 2763** 食品中农药最大残留限量 | China's pesticide MRL standard, ~2,000+ limits, far broader than most origins expect. | **We have never run a residue panel.** Genuine detention risk. |

**A fourth gate, added 2026-10 — aflatoxin is not currently tracked in this plan.**

The plan carries OTA (§1.2, B4) and heavy metals, but **no aflatoxin B1 line**, and China enforces this one hard. In 2021 a Kellogg's cocoa cereal import tested at **31.9 μg/kg** B1 against GB 2761's **20 μg/kg** limit and drew **¥18.37m in fines, ¥450k illegal proceeds seized, and 27,672 boxes confiscated**. The regulator identified the mechanisms as contaminated raw material, poor production hygiene, and — directly relevant to us — **"包装密封不严、储运条件控制不当"** (inadequate packaging seal, improper storage/transport control).

Brazil → China is a long, humid, containerised journey on a susceptible commodity. Given metals already test `<0.025 mg/kg` with ≥20× headroom against China's lead limit, **aflatoxin is plausibly the larger real exposure** — while in the EU the same cacao is a cadmium story.

- **Unchecked:** whether GB 2761 carries a 可可制品-specific B1 row. The Kellogg's case applied the 玉米制品 limit because 可可球 is a cereal matrix, so the applicable row for cocoa is **not established**.
- **Action:** free desk check (item 3 of `CACAO_TCM_APPLICATION.md` §10), then aflatoxin B1 + moisture on actual lots plus Oscar's storage/transit protocol.

Everything else — flavanols, theobromine, and **cadmium and arsenic, which GB 2762 does not limit for cocoa at all** (§3) — is **differentiation**, not permission. The gates above are permission.

### 1.2 Two gaps this report exposes — the first one is strategically serious

**Gap A — the certificate does not name the farm.**

The report names the **requester** as `Black King` (contact Matheus Reis Pereira), the matrix as `Nibs de cacau`, and an address on Avenida Tancredo Neves, Ilhéus/BA — **a commercial address, not Oscar's farm**. A lab certificate that cannot itself identify the farm is materially weaker than the story requires — a Chinese importer asking "prove this is that farm's cacao" would get our internal mapping, not the laboratory's word.

Retroactively, `LAP-339.2025.B` cannot be amended — it can only be paired with our internal lineage record and a documented explanation.

**Gap B — it is nibs, not powder.**

Cadmium and other metals **concentrate with cocoa-solids content**. A clean nibs result does **not** automatically transfer to cocoa powder or to a high-percentage chocolate bar, because those are different matrices at different solids concentrations. Any powder claim requires a powder-lot test.

**Net effect on Phase 1:** B3 (metals) drops from "discovery" to "confirm on powder". The genuine unknowns remain **B1 flavanols at DP 1–10**, **B2 epicatechin**, **B4 ochratoxin A**, **B5 theobromine/caffeine**, and later **C1 pesticide residues** — none of which this report covers.

### 1.3 A1 RESOLVED — there is no 药典 entry for 可可, and the folk 性味归经 is unsourced

**Status: closed.** This was the question gating the whole "translate a CIC lab test into TCM" idea.

#### The answer

Cross-checked against the **official 《中国药典》 一部 table of contents** (2015 edition, hosted by `cnppa.org` — China's pharmacopoeia promotion body, i.e. an official document, not a health-media summary):

- **No 可可 or 可可豆 entry anywhere in the 一部 list.**
- **2020 and 2025 revision additions checked too** — the 2025 edition added 159 species (28 of them 中药); cocoa is not among them.

> **Gate holds.** Cacao has **no TCM status**, therefore there is nothing to translate a lab test *into*. The premise of the original idea fails at the first step, not at the last.

**Evidence grade, stated precisely** — because this determines how hard we can lean on it:

| Claim | Confidence |
|---|---|
| No 可可 entry in the 一部 contents | **High** — a real negative check against an official document, not an absence-of-results inference |
| Not a full-text search of 《中华本草》 or 《中药大辞典》 | **Caveat** — neither our contact nor this autopilot has that access |
| `ydz.chp.org.cn` page citation | **Unavailable** — login-walled paid query system |

The conclusion "not in the pharmacopoeia" is supported. A page-level citation is not producible, and saying otherwise would overstate it.

#### Correction to one argument, because it will get reused

The research came back with: *cocoa is a post-Columbian import, therefore a classical 本草 citation **structurally cannot** exist.*

**Direction right, proof too strong.** The 本草 tradition repeatedly absorbs later imports and non-classical substances — 《本草纲目拾遗》 (赵学敏, 1765) exists precisely to add what the 纲目 missed, and the 纲目 itself carries categories for 造酿 and foreign materia. "It is an import" is therefore **strong supporting context, not proof**.

The actual proof is the negative lookup: a targeted search for a cocoa entry in 《本草纲目拾遗》 — the *exact* citation popular Chinese health media reaches for when a foreign food gets a TCM gloss — found **no citation trail at all**.

#### The folk attribution is confirmed as internet copy

Chinese health media assign cocoa a confident 性味归经, and they **disagree with each other**:

| Source | Claim |
|---|---|
| Baidu Health | 性温，味甘、微苦，**归心、脾经**，提神醒脑、抗氧化、促进消化 |
| 東周網 (HK column) | 归**心、脾、肾**经，鼓舞心脏阳气、促进血液循环、疏通经络 |

Both cite nothing. Decisively, the aggregator sites repeating the 归心脾经 version **admit it in their own text**:

> 「原始文献出处需要进一步查证」— *the original source still needs verification.*

That is the tell: a sourced claim states its source, whereas an unsourced one says the source "needs further verification" while the claim itself keeps propagating.

> **Operational rule: never cite this. 性味 language is tone and narrative only — zero regulatory, zero scholarly authority.** Copying it would import an unsourced claim plus regulatory exposure, and a knowledgeable Chinese buyer spots it immediately.

#### What survives — the EU ↔ Chinese narrative convergence

The one genuinely reusable finding is that the folk Chinese description and the **EU-authorised** wording describe the same physiology in different vocabularies:

| Chinese folk (unsourced) | EU Reg. 851/2013 (authorised) |
|---|---|
| 促进血液循环 | *helps maintain blood flow* |
| 疏通经络 | *maintains the elasticity of blood vessels* |
| 鼓舞心脏阳气 | *(same vascular-terms claim)* |

So we never have to invent a bridge between the two markets — **one true thing can be said in two vocabularies**. But the asymmetry must be respected:

- In the **EU**: the claim is lawful, but only at **200 mg cocoa flavanols/day, DP 1–10** (see the bibliography doc).
- In **China**: cacao is **普通食品**, so this cannot become a 功效 claim at all. The folk wording has no source; the EU wording has no Chinese legal equivalent.

**Use it as narrative, in both markets, claim it in neither beyond what is authorised.**

#### Still open

The three-part question put to our China-side contact returned **Q1 and Q2 researched; Q3 unanswered** — namely what is actually *sayable* as 普通食品 versus 保健食品, and whether **地方特色食品** or narrative on-pack copy offers a narrow lawful path. That is now the last positioning question standing.

### 1.4 A1 addendum — TCM re-enters as a *formulation framework*, not a claim

§1.3 above is correct and is not reopened: there is no 药典 entry, the folk 性味归经 is unsourced and must never be cited, and TCM cannot be a regulatory unlock or a health claim in China.

But §1.3 answered only *"can TCM be a claim?"* It left a narrower question open: **can TCM enter through the recipe instead of the label?**

It can, via **配伍** (formulation pairing) and **炮制** (processing) — both recipe activities, and a recipe is not a claim. Cocoa is not 药食同源; its classic partners are; and 普通食品 may legally contain 药食同源 ingredients. So TCM can drive the formulation while the pack carries only verifiable facts.

Full framework in **`CACAO_TCM_APPLICATION.md`**. Two constraints carry over and are absolute there:

- The §1.3 rule still binds — 性味 derivation stays internal, never on a pack.
- §7 do-not #7 still holds — **do not lead the China pitch with TCM.** Lead with compliance and provenance. TCM is a formulation brief and a 1:1 conversation layer, not a pitch.

The 药食同源 partner membership is **verified**; the 性味/归经 derivation and the 证→product matrix are **sophia synthesis and need practitioner review** before leaving the design brief.

## 3. The cadmium question — RESOLVED, and it flips the EU vs China risk map

**Status: closed, and the answer is stronger than the hypothesis.**

### 3.1 What GB 2762 actually says about cocoa

Read directly from the GB 2762-2022 tables (two independent PDF copies extracted and cross-checked), and confirmed against the GB 2762-2025 changes:

| Contaminant | GB 2762 limit for **cocoa products, chocolate and chocolate products** |
|---|---|
| **Lead (Pb)** | **0.5 mg/kg** — a real limit, appears in Table 1 |
| **Cadmium (Cd)** | **No limit.** No cocoa row exists in Table 2 |
| **Arsenic (As)** | **No limit.** No cocoa row exists in Table 4 |
| **Nickel (Ni)** | No limit |

This is not truncation in a search snippet. The cadmium table (Table 2) was read in full: it covers grains, vegetables, fruit, edible fungi, beans, **nuts and seeds**, meat, aquatic products, eggs, condiments, salt, beverages and infant foods — and the only nuts entry is **peanuts at 0.5 mg/kg**. **Cocoa is not there.** Meanwhile cocoa appears explicitly as its own named row in the **lead** table and in the **arsenic**-context list. The absence of a cocoa cadmium row is therefore a deliberate omission, not a gap in extraction.

**The 2025 revision does not change this.** Five sources incl. the official NHC release (2025-09-25, effective **2026-09-02**) enumerate the six changes: 肉类干制品 折算 + 铅/镉/总砷/铬 判定统一, 包装饮用水, 食用燕窝 亚硝酸盐, 食用燕窝 食品类别 — and **no cocoa change**. Note: our earlier research contact stated this as *"GB 2762 cadmium for cocoa is 0.5 mg/kg"*. **That figure is the LEAD limit.** The 0.5 in the cocoa row sits in the lead column (Table 1); the cadmium table has no cocoa row at all. The correct conclusion happens to be even better for us than the mistaken one, but it needed the correction.

### 3.2 The flipped risk map

| | EU (Reg. 488/2014) | China (GB 2762) |
|---|---|---|
| Chocolate ≥ 50 % dry cocoa solids | **0.80 mg/kg Cd** | no Cd limit |
| Chocolate 30–50 % solids / milk choc ≥ 30 % | **0.30 mg/kg Cd** | no Cd limit |
| Milk chocolate < 30 % solids | **0.10 mg/kg Cd** | no Cd limit |
| **Cocoa powder sold to consumers** | **0.60 mg/kg Cd** | no Cd limit |
| Cocoa beans as such | not covered (limits are on finished product) | not covered |

> **This inverts the assumption the plan was built on.** The EU — not China — is the binding constraint on our cadmium exposure. China gates us on **lead** only, and only at 0.5 mg/kg, where we have ≥ 20× headroom.

**An important consequence for product form:** because the EU sets the cocoa *powder* limit at 0.60 and the bean/nib level is governed only indirectly through finished-product limits, **powder is our most cadmium-exposed SKU in the EU and our least gated in China**. If a powder result ever comes back high, the viable route is **nibs/liquor into China** rather than powder into Europe. That is a real strategic option the metal data now supports — and the reason the nibs-vs-powder distinction in §1.2 mattered.

### 3.3 What we must still not conclude

**"China has no Cd limit" ≠ "China is lax, so ship whatever."** Three reasons the discipline stands:

1. **Absence of a limit is not absence of enforcement.** 监督抽检 can still flag a 重金属 item under 食品安全法 general-safety provisions, and rejection at port is a commercial loss regardless of whether a numeric ML was breached.
2. **A repeat customer can impose its own spec.** Nutrada's practitioner note is blunt: *"some buyers will not accept beans that have more than 0.3 mg per kilogram"* — stricter than China's law and stricter than the EU chocolate ML. Private spec, not law, can close the door.
3. **The EU number is still ours to meet.** One measured result has to satisfy the strictest market we sell into. An unmeasured metal is a liability everywhere.

**So: verify the rule (done), and test the metal regardless.** The measured < 0.025 mg/kg from `LAP-339.2025.B` is what makes both markets safe; the *rule* merely decides which one is closeable today.

## 4. On the "中国医学研究所 equivalent" — who to actually approach

There is no single equivalent, and the premise ("translate a lab test for TCM") does not match what any of these bodies do. Here is the honest map:

| Body | Chinese name | What it actually does | Useful to us? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences — Institute of Chinese Materia Medica** | 中国中医科学院中药研究所 | The authoritative body on materia medica: 性味归经, 本草文献, quality standards for 中药材 | **Only if** A1 shows an existing 可可 entry to interpret. Cannot *create* TCM status for a non-pharmacopoeia item on demand. |
| **CAMS — Institute of Medicinal Plant Development** | 中国医学科学院药用植物研究所 | Medicinal plant chemistry, pharmacology | Marginal — cacao is not a medicinal plant in the Chinese system |
| **China National Center for Food Safety Risk Assessment** | 国家食品安全风险评估中心 (CFSA) | Food safety risk assessment underpinning GB standards | **Indirectly** — the body whose science sits behind GB 2762/2763 |
| **National Institute for Nutrition and Health, China CDC** | 中国疾病预防控制中心营养与健康所 | Nutrition science, dietary guidance | Plausible partner for *nutrition* framing (not TCM) |
| **Chinese Nutrition Society** | 中国营养学会 | Dietary guidelines, professional credibility | Plausible for science-communication credibility |
| **SAMR** | 国家市场监督管理总局 | Regulates 保健食品 registration | The gatekeeper if we ever go Phase 4 |
| **NIFDC** | 中国食品药品检定研究院 | National labs for food/drug testing, method standards | Reference point for acceptable test methods |

**The honest conclusion:** the thing you want — a credible Chinese institution blessing cacao in TCM language — is **not a translation service that exists**. The closest genuine move is:

1. Establish whether a 可可 entry exists in the materia medica literature (**A1**).
2. If it does, engage a **TCM-literate writer or a 中药学 academic** to interpret it accurately — cheap, and honest.
3. If it does not, drop the TCM frame and build the China story on **食品安全 compliance + verifiable provenance**, which is what Chinese premium consumers actually respond to for imported food.

> Strategic judgement: **TCM is a narrative layer, not a regulatory unlock.** It would not move a single regulatory gate in §0. The gates are GACC, GB 2762 and GB 2763. Spend the first dollars there.

## 5. Where Oscar's farm is genuinely unique in China

Do not lead with TCM. Lead with these, in this order:

### 5.1 Per-unit auditable provenance — the real moat
China has an acute, well-documented consumer anxiety about **food fraud and mislabelled origin**. The standard countermeasure in the market is brand trust and third-party seals.

**We have something structurally better: a QR code on the physical bag that resolves to an immutable, per-unit lineage record** — farm, tree/plot, vintage, fermentation, and (once Phase 1 lands) its measured chemistry. No importer of cocoa into China offers unit-level auditable provenance, because most are trading houses reselling fungible lots.

The Blueprint-style competitor has a PDF certificate per *product line*. We can have a record per *bag*. In a market that distrusts labels, that is the differentiating claim.

### 5.2 Measured chemistry, published before asked
Flavanols at DP 1–10, epicatechin, metals against **both** GB 2762 and EU limits, OTA. Publishing a number that is *not* flattering is a credibility asset — it is the behaviour Chinese premium buyers are least accustomed to from foreign suppliers.

### 5.3 Single-farm, single-origin, small-lot
Commodity cocoa is blended by definition. Oscar's is not. Rarity plus verifiability is a coherent premium story in any market.

### 5.4 Agroforestry / cabruca system
The Bahian **cabruca** system (cacao under native Atlantic Forest shade) is a genuine environmental story — biodiversity, shade-grown, low-input. **Caveat: do not make carbon-credit or offset claims without measurement.** Anecdote is fine; a number needs a methodology.

### 5.5 What does *not* travel
- **Ceremonial cacao culture.** China has essentially no cacao ceremony tradition. Lane A is unlikely to transfer; the ritual framing has no local referent.
- **The "heart-opening" / spiritual vocabulary.** No cultural landing zone, and it collides with advertising rules.
- **The EU EFSA wording.** No legal standing in China whatsoever.
- **"70% dark chocolate" logic.** As established elsewhere, cocoa percentage is not a flavanol proxy — and it would be indefensible to a technical buyer anyway.

## 7. Explicit do-nots

1. **Do not claim 药食同源.** Cocoa is not on the list. A false claim gets shipments detained and the importer blacklisted.
2. **Do not port the EU EFSA wording to China.** It has no standing and invites scrutiny.
3. **Do not make any 保健食品-style functional claim** on 普通食品 packaging.
4. **Do not assume Brazil is low-cadmium.** Test it.
5. **Do not assume GACC route is Group 2.** Verify the HS code first.
6. **Do not start 保健食品 registration** before there is demonstrated demand — cocoa is not even on the permitted-ingredient list.
7. **Do not lead the China pitch with TCM.** Lead with compliance and provable provenance; TCM is at best a garnish and at worst a liability.
8. **Do not assume Lane A travels.** It probably does not.

## 8. One-paragraph summary

The Chinese market does not need Oscar's cacao translated into TCM; it needs it proved safe, legally labelled, and registered with customs — and TCM has no regulatory lever to pull at all, because cacao sits on neither 药食同源 nor the 保健食品 ingredient list, and 性味归经 is doctrine rather than something a laboratory can output. The three gates that actually decide this are GACC Decree 248 facility registration, GB 2762 contaminants (lead confirmed at 0.5 mg/kg, with **no cadmium or arsenic limit for cocoa at all** — now confirmed against both the 2022 and 2025 texts (§3)), and GB 2763 pesticide residues, which we have never tested. Phase 0 is free desk research that resolves most of the strategic uncertainty, and Phase 1 is a single sub-USD-1,000 lab panel on one 2025 lot that either opens the lane or closes it honestly. Our genuine uniqueness in China is not a philosophical frame but a physical object: a QR code on each bag resolving to a per-unit, auditable lineage record, in a market whose deepest food anxiety is that labels lie — and that advantage serves the mission directly, because a certified, premium-priced, single-farm Brazilian cacao lot is what makes 10,000 hectares of Amazonian agroforestry pay for the farmer.
