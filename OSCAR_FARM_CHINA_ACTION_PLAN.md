## 0. Two headline answers, up front

### Answer 1 — TCM is the wrong door, and the premise has a category error in it

Cocoa appears on **neither** of China's two gating lists. Verified against both:

| List | What it controls | Cocoa present? |
|---|---|---|
| **药食同源目录** (food-medicine homology, 106 items) | Whether a substance may be positioned as both food and medicine | ❌ **Absent** |
| **可用于保健食品的物品名单** (卫法监发51号 — health-food permitted ingredients) | The only ingredients a 保健食品 may contain | ❌ **Absent** |

So cacao's only lawful identity in China is **普通食品 (ordinary food)**. That means: no 保健食品 "blue-hat" claim, no 药食同源 positioning, no functional claim of any kind, absent a multi-year registration we have no reason to attempt yet.

**But the deeper issue is conceptual.** You asked for a lab test to be *translated* into TCM. That cannot be done, by anyone:

> **性味归经** (nature / flavour / meridian attribution) is assigned by **doctrine and Pharmacopoeia entry**, not derived from analytical chemistry. A laboratory measures a substance. It does not classify it. There is no institute — in China or anywhere — that performs "lab result → TCM category" as a service, because that is not a chemical determination.

So the useful version of the question is narrower and *is* answerable:

- **Does 《中国药典》 / 《中华本草》 / 《中药大辞典》 already contain a 可可 (cacao) entry** with a stated 性味归经?
  - **If yes** → an existing TCM vocabulary hook exists, and we can *use* it as cultural narrative (never as a health claim).
  - **If no** → there is no TCM status to borrow. Any 性味 language would be our own invention, which is a marketing choice with regulatory risk attached.

This is **Task A1** below. It is free to answer and it settles the whole TCM question.

### Answer 2 — the real gates are three, and all three are bureaucratic, not scientific

| # | Gate | Requirement | Cocoa's exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **GACC Decree 248** (effective 2022-01-01) | Every overseas food **manufacturer, processor and storage facility** exporting to China must register with the General Administration of Customs. The **China Registration Number must be printed on both inner and outer packaging**. Registration is only valid for the specific HS + quarantine-code combination registered. | Cocoa is not in GACC's Group 1 (the 18 categories needing home-authority recommendation, e.g. meat/dairy/aquatic); it most likely falls in **Group 2 — self-application**. **Verify before assuming.** |
| **2** | **GB 2762** 食品中污染物限量 | Lead limit for **可可制品、巧克力和巧克力制品以及糖果** = **0.5 mg/kg (as Pb)** — confirmed against the standard. | **Cadmium status is UNRESOLVED and important.** See §3. |
| **3** | **GB 2763** 食品中农药最大残留限量 | China's pesticide MRL standard, ~2,000+ limits, far broader than most origins expect. | **We have never run a residue panel.** Genuine detention risk. |

Everything else — flavanols, theobromine, cadmium *if* it turns out to be unregulated for cocoa — is **differentiation**, not permission. The gates above are permission.

## 2. The discovery list — what we actually need to find out

Organised by leverage per unit of cost. **Phase 0 is free and resolves most of the strategic uncertainty.**

### Phase 0 — Desk research (free; do this first)

| # | Question | Why it decides something |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Does 《中国药典》/《中华本草》/《中药大辞典》 contain a **可可** entry with 性味归经? | Settles the entire TCM question. If no entry, there is no TCM status to translate to — full stop. |
| **A2** | Does **GB 2762-2022 / the 2025 revision** set a **cadmium** limit for 可可制品 / 巧克力? | If **no**, China is materially softer on cocoa Cd than the EU (which has had Cd max levels for chocolate since 2019). That is a strategic asymmetry in our favour and reframes the whole risk map. **Highest-value single question in this plan.** |
| **A3** | What is the correct product classification for our goods — **可可豆 (beans), 可可粉 (powder), 可可脂 (butter), 巧克力**? | Each maps to a different GB 2762 row, a different HS code, and possibly a different GACC registration route. |
| **A4** | Does our HS + product category fall in GACC **Group 1** (needs MAPA recommendation) or **Group 2** (self-application)? | Group 2 is weeks; Group 1 is months and depends on a Brazilian authority. Completely different project plans. |
| **A5** | Does China give **GI protection** to Brazil's **"Sul da Bahia"** cacao Indicação Geográfica, or is there Brazil–China GI mutual recognition? | If yes, an origin-protection moat is available and cheap. If no, origin protection is only de facto. |
| **D1** | Which Chinese institutions actually do this work? (see §4) | So we don't approach a body that cannot help. |
| **E1** | Who currently imports specialty/fine cacao into China? (craft chocolate importers, Shanghai/Beijing speciality food) | Tells us whether a channel exists or must be built. |
| **E2** | Is there any **cacao ceremony** scene in mainland China? | Determines whether Lane A travels at all. Current expectation: **it does not.** |
| **E3** | What do imported cocoa powders sell for in China, and at what positioning? | Price anchor before we spend on compliance. |

### Phase 1 — One cheap lab panel, one lot (approx. USD 300–800)

Send a **single composite sample from one 2025 Oscar lot** for:

| # | Test | Standard/method | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **Cocoa flavanols, total AND DP 1–10** | HPLC (same method class Barry Callebaut used) | DP 1–10 is the **exact spec of the only authorised claim in any major jurisdiction** (EU 851/2013, 200 mg). Total-only is not enough. |
| **B2** | **(−)-epicatechin** | HPLC | The single best marker of flavanol integrity; correlates ~R²=0.96 with total flavanols. |
| **B3** | **Pb, Cd, As, Hg, Cu** | ICP-MS | Compare **side by side** against GB 2762 (China), EU 488/2014, and ANVISA. One number, three market verdicts. |
| **B4** | **Ochratoxin A (OTA)** | HPLC-FLD | Both China and the EU regulate OTA in cocoa. **Never tested by us.** A positive result would be a hard stop. |
| **B5** | **Theobromine + caffeine** | HPLC | Dosage/safety framing; also a **labelling** input, and relevant to sleep/HRV-tracking buyers. |
| **B6** | **Microbiological panel** | Standard plating | Basic import prerequisite. |

> **Why one lot, not the farm:** a certificate is only honest if it describes the thing it's attached to. One lot gives us one defensible claim and tells us whether the *idea* works at all before we scale testing.

### Phase 2 — The wider compliance panel

| # | Test | Why it's separate |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **GB 2763 pesticide residue panel** (multi-residue, China MRLs) | Broad and expensive. The biggest unmeasured detention risk in the whole plan. |
| **C2** | **CIC laudo** on a representative lot (flavanols DP 1–10, epicatechin, metals, microbiology, fermentation index) | Brazilian institutional credibility + the science institution that publishes on exactly Bahian cocoa bioactive↔sensory. |
| **C3** | **Fermentation index / degree of fermentation** | Lets us *evidence* the craft claim, not just assert it. Connects to CIC's own published work. |

### Phase 3 — Commercial (only after 1–2 come back clean)

- GACC Decree 248 registration — likely via **Coopercabruca** as the registered Brazilian facility/exporter
- **Chinese label** to GB 7718 (prepackaged food labelling) + GB 28050 (nutrition labelling) — full Chinese text, and **no health claim is permitted**
- Identify and contract an importer of record
- Confirm the China Registration Number appears on **inner and outer** packaging

### Phase 4 — Only if demand is validated

- **保健食品 ("blue-hat") registration** — multi-year, product-specific, expensive, and requires the ingredient to be on the permitted list. **Cocoa is not.** This would require a 新食品原料 or 保健食品原料 route first. Do not start here.

## 3. The unresolved cadmium question — the highest-value unknown

This deserves its own section because it flips the strategy.

**What I confirmed:** GB 2762 sets **lead at 0.5 mg/kg** for 可可制品、巧克力和巧克力制品以及糖果.

**What I could not confirm:** a **cadmium** limit for cocoa products. In the GB 2762 cadmium table I retrieved, the rows cover grains, vegetables, fruits, edible fungi, legumes, nuts/seeds, meat, aquatic products, eggs, condiments and beverages — 可可 does **not** visibly appear, whereas it clearly appears in the lead table. That may be truncation in my source, so **treat it as an open question, not a finding.**

**Why it matters enormously if true:**

| | EU | China (if no cocoa Cd limit) |
|---|---|---|
| Cocoa Cd | Max levels in force for chocolate since 2019 | No cocoa-specific limit |
| Our exposure | **Hard gate** — a high-Cd lot is unsaleable in Europe | Not a compliance gate |

We already flagged (in `CACAO_IN_QUANTIFIED_BIOHACKING.md`) that **Brazil-specific cadmium data is thin** and that the Cd literature is dominated by Ecuador/Peru/Colombia. If China does not gate cocoa on cadmium, then **China may be the market where our cadmium risk is least binding** — which is an uncomfortable but genuinely strategic insight, and one we should verify before acting on either way.

**Do not interpret this as "China is lax so we can sell dirty cacao."** It means: (a) verify the rule, and (b) test the metal regardless, because a measured number is our asset in *every* market and an unmeasured one is a liability in the EU even if not in China.

## 4. On the "中国医学研究所 equivalent" — who to actually approach

There is no single equivalent, and the premise ("translate a lab test for TCM") does not match what any of these bodies do. Here is the honest map:

| Body | Chinese name | What it actually does | Useful to us? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences — Institute of Chinese Materia Medica** | 中国中医科学院中药研究所 | The authoritative body on materia medica: 性味归经, 本草文献, quality standards for 中药材 | **Only if** A1 shows an existing 可可 entry to interpret. Cannot *create* TCM status for a non-pharmacopoeia item on demand. |
| **CAMS — Institute of Medicinal Plant Development** | 中国医学科学院药用植物研究所 | Medicinal plant chemistry, pharmacology | Marginal — cacao is not a medicinal plant in the Chinese system |
| **China National Center for Food Safety Risk Assessment** | 国家食品安全风险评估中心 (CFSA) | Food safety risk assessment underpinning GB standards | **Indirectly** — the body whose science sits behind GB 2762/2763 |
| **National Institute for Nutrition and Health, China CDC** | 中国疾病预防控制中心营养与健康所 | Nutrition science, dietary guidance | Plausible partner for *nutrition* framing (not TCM) |
| **Chinese Nutrition Society** | 中国营养学会 | Dietary guidelines, professional credibility | Plausible for science-communication credibility |
| **SAMR** | 国家市场监督管理总局 | Regulates 保健食品 registration | The gatekeeper if we ever go Phase 4 |
| **NIFDC** | 中国食品药品检定研究院 | National labs for food/drug testing, method standards | Reference point for acceptable test methods |

**The honest conclusion:** the thing you want — a credible Chinese institution blessing cacao in TCM language — is **not a translation service that exists**. The closest genuine move is:

1. Establish whether a 可可 entry exists in the materia medica literature (**A1**).
2. If it does, engage a **TCM-literate writer or a 中药学 academic** to interpret it accurately — cheap, and honest.
3. If it does not, drop the TCM frame and build the China story on **食品安全 compliance + verifiable provenance**, which is what Chinese premium consumers actually respond to for imported food.

> Strategic judgement: **TCM is a narrative layer, not a regulatory unlock.** It would not move a single regulatory gate in §0. The gates are GACC, GB 2762 and GB 2763. Spend the first dollars there.

## 5. Where Oscar's farm is genuinely unique in China

Do not lead with TCM. Lead with these, in this order:

### 5.1 Per-unit auditable provenance — the real moat
China has an acute, well-documented consumer anxiety about **food fraud and mislabelled origin**. The standard countermeasure in the market is brand trust and third-party seals.

**We have something structurally better: a QR code on the physical bag that resolves to an immutable, per-unit lineage record** — farm, tree/plot, vintage, fermentation, and (once Phase 1 lands) its measured chemistry. No importer of cocoa into China offers unit-level auditable provenance, because most are trading houses reselling fungible lots.

The Blueprint-style competitor has a PDF certificate per *product line*. We can have a record per *bag*. In a market that distrusts labels, that is the differentiating claim.

### 5.2 Measured chemistry, published before asked
Flavanols at DP 1–10, epicatechin, metals against **both** GB 2762 and EU limits, OTA. Publishing a number that is *not* flattering is a credibility asset — it is the behaviour Chinese premium buyers are least accustomed to from foreign suppliers.

### 5.3 Single-farm, single-origin, small-lot
Commodity cocoa is blended by definition. Oscar's is not. Rarity plus verifiability is a coherent premium story in any market.

### 5.4 Agroforestry / cabruca system
The Bahian **cabruca** system (cacao under native Atlantic Forest shade) is a genuine environmental story — biodiversity, shade-grown, low-input. **Caveat: do not make carbon-credit or offset claims without measurement.** Anecdote is fine; a number needs a methodology.

### 5.5 What does *not* travel
- **Ceremonial cacao culture.** China has essentially no cacao ceremony tradition. Lane A is unlikely to transfer; the ritual framing has no local referent.
- **The "heart-opening" / spiritual vocabulary.** No cultural landing zone, and it collides with advertising rules.
- **The EU EFSA wording.** No legal standing in China whatsoever.
- **"70% dark chocolate" logic.** As established elsewhere, cocoa percentage is not a flavanol proxy — and it would be indefensible to a technical buyer anyway.

## 7. Explicit do-nots

1. **Do not claim 药食同源.** Cocoa is not on the list. A false claim gets shipments detained and the importer blacklisted.
2. **Do not port the EU EFSA wording to China.** It has no standing and invites scrutiny.
3. **Do not make any 保健食品-style functional claim** on 普通食品 packaging.
4. **Do not assume Brazil is low-cadmium.** Test it.
5. **Do not assume GACC route is Group 2.** Verify the HS code first.
6. **Do not start 保健食品 registration** before there is demonstrated demand — cocoa is not even on the permitted-ingredient list.
7. **Do not lead the China pitch with TCM.** Lead with compliance and provable provenance; TCM is at best a garnish and at worst a liability.
8. **Do not assume Lane A travels.** It probably does not.

## 8. One-paragraph summary

The Chinese market does not need Oscar's cacao translated into TCM; it needs it proved safe, legally labelled, and registered with customs — and TCM has no regulatory lever to pull at all, because cacao sits on neither 药食同源 nor the 保健食品 ingredient list, and 性味归经 is doctrine rather than something a laboratory can output. The three gates that actually decide this are GACC Decree 248 facility registration, GB 2762 contaminants (lead confirmed at 0.5 mg/kg; **cadmium for cocoa unverified and possibly absent — the highest-value unknown in the plan**), and GB 2763 pesticide residues, which we have never tested. Phase 0 is free desk research that resolves most of the strategic uncertainty, and Phase 1 is a single sub-USD-1,000 lab panel on one 2025 lot that either opens the lane or closes it honestly. Our genuine uniqueness in China is not a philosophical frame but a physical object: a QR code on each bag resolving to a per-unit, auditable lineage record, in a market whose deepest food anxiety is that labels lie — and that advantage serves the mission directly, because a certified, premium-priced, single-farm Brazilian cacao lot is what makes 10,000 hectares of Amazonian agroforestry pay for the farmer.
