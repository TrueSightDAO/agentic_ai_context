## 0. Retraction — I overstated my McCann critique

In PR #1839 §2 I wrote that Envoy's decomposition — *"Truth = impossible to deny / Well = impossible to ignore / Told = impossible to avoid"* — "is not what the framework is."

**That was too harsh, and partly wrong.**

- McCann Canada's CCO **Jordan Doucette** describes McCann's framework in almost exactly those words: *"the framework of 'impossible to deny, impossible to ignore, impossible to avoid'… you can look at a piece of work… and you apply that framework to it."*
- McCann's global leadership use the same triad: *"Truth is impossible to deny. When you tell it well, it's impossible to ignore. And when it's out in the world and has an impact, it should be impossible to forget."*

So **the triad is genuine McCann language**, not an invention, and Envoy's three-stage mapping is properly grounded. I was wrong to call it a misreading. Corrected.

### What survives of the critique — narrower

One point still holds, and it is the commercially load-bearing one:

1. **In McCann's frame, "Truth" is a *human* truth.** Their own formulation is *"We unlock **human truths** and turn those truths into compelling stories."* Envoy maps Truth onto *"the actual, checkable fact"* — a **product** fact. In McCann's vocabulary, verifiable provenance is closer to what makes a truth *undeniable* (an RTB), not the truth itself.
2. **McCann explicitly warn against exactly this substitution:** *"You move the market by moving people. And you're not going to move people by just telling them why **functional superiority** should command a price premium."* (verbatim, Media Marketing interview). Making verifiable provenance the foundation is a functional-superiority argument.

**Net position after the retraction:** Envoy's *framework* is sound; the *content* assigned to "Truth" is a product fact where McCann require a human truth. That is a refinement of one cell, not a rejection of the model — which is how I should have put it the first time.

## 1. The ¥88 justification has a directional error

v2 §6 states:

> *"sits just below the field-confirmed Café-Tasse comparable (**¥1,760/kg vs ¥1,250/kg**) — a defensible premium"*

**These two clauses contradict each other on their face.** ¥1,760 is not below ¥1,250. It is **41% above it**. And "just" understates a 41% gap.

| | ¥/kg | vs Café-Tasse |
|---|---:|---:|
| Café-Tasse 77%, 20 g @ ¥25 | 1,250 | 1.00× |
| **Agroverse @ ¥88 / 50 g** | **1,760** | **1.41× — above, not below** |

**The fix is one word plus one number**, and the argument is still fine: ¥88 is a **41% premium above** the only craft comparable, justified by claims Café-Tasse does not make (single estate + vintage + lot + assay + per-bar QR). A 41% premium for a materially better-documented product is *defensible*. Describing it as "just below" is not, and it's the kind of line a retail buyer checks in five seconds.

**One related internal inconsistency:** v2 says above **¥98** the bar "is compared directly against Godiva without the brand equity to support it." But the same document's own comparable table lists **Godiva 85% at ¥159/100g** — i.e. **¥79.50 per 50 g**. At ¥88 (¥176/100g) the bar is *already* above Godiva's per-100 g price. So the ¥98 "Godiva ceiling" cannot be doing the work claimed. Either the comparable is wrong or the ceiling reasoning is — worth resolving, because both feed §6.

## 2. "Every SKU below ¥230/kg is a single bar" is false on v2's own table

v2 §3, and repeated as the evidence base for §4's recommendation:

> *"every SKU below ¥230/kg is a single bar, one flavor. Every SKU above ¥1,000/kg is either a curated multi-origin/multi-flavor box or a small tasting-portion multi-pack."*

The second sentence is **true and useful**. The first is **false**, with three counterexamples sitting in v2's own ladder:

| ¥/kg | SKU | Format | Store |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 198.21 | Bouchard 低糖 80% (3 flavors) | **multi-flavor box** | Sam's |
| 199.60 | Member's Mark 什锦比利时 Ballotin | **curated assortment box** | Sam's |
| 202.59 | Lindt LINDOR 软心甄选 | **curated selection box** | Sam's |

And in the other direction, **single bars run well above ¥230/kg** — CHOCDAY 98% at **588.89** and Beryl's 99% at **537.04**. So format does **not** track price below the top rung; the two overlap across the whole mid-tier.

### Why this matters rather than being pedantic

The clean, defensible version of the claim is only this:

> **Above ¥1,000/kg there is no single-flavour single bar — the top rung is occupied exclusively by curated boxes and tasting multi-packs.**

That is true, and it still supports the discovery-box recommendation. But the *reason* it supports it changes: the box is right because **the top of the ladder is a gifting register, and gifting is boxed** — not because "boxes are what premium means." As stated, the claim is falsifiable from v2's own Page 1, and a Chinese retail buyer reading the ladder will find the three counterexamples immediately.

**Recommendation status:** the 4-bar discovery box **survives** §2 correction — it is supported by Café-Tasse's tasting format, by 卡卡星座's collectible set, and by the gifting logic. It is *not* supported by a price-format law that doesn't hold.

## 3. The Meiji ladder row is arithmetically inconsistent

v2 §3 ladder, row 6:

> `198.67 | Meiji 超纯黑巧克力 50–70% | single bar, 1 flavor | 75g | ¥18.90 | NB`

**Check:** ¥18.90 ÷ 0.075 kg = **¥252.00/kg**, not ¥198.67. ¥198.67 is exactly ¥14.90 ÷ 0.075 — the **NB shelf tag**. ¥18.90 was the **blt** tag.

The row has merged two stores' prices. Correctly split:

| Product | Size | Price | ¥/kg | Store |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Meiji 超纯黑巧 70% | 75 g | ¥14.90 | **198.67** | 超盒算NB |
| Meiji 超纯黑巧 70% (委内瑞拉) | 75 g | ¥18.90 | **252.00** | blt |
| Meiji 黑巧 50% (多米尼加) | 75 g | ¥18.90 | **252.00** | blt |

This matters twice over. It corrupts the ¥/kg figure in the ladder, **and** it blurs the two distinct findings built on these tags:

- **The zero-premium finding** is a *within-blt* fact: 50% and 70%, same 75 g, both ¥18.90 → different cocoa %, different origin claim, **zero** price difference.
- **The channel delta** is a *cross-store* fact: the same 70% SKU at ¥14.90 in NB vs ¥18.90 in blt → **+27%**.

v2's note (*"this SKU is 50% cacao, not the 70% figure used in some analysis turns"*) shows the confusion was noticed but the row wasn't corrected. Both findings survive; the ladder row needs splitting.

## 5. The regulatory section contradicts itself — and the resolution is precise

v2 §2 says, correctly, that **普通食品 may carry zero functional claims**, and identifies CHOCDAY's 膳食纤维有助于维持正常的肠道功能 as *"a regulated function claim, the exact category this section says is closed."*

**But CHOCDAY is on shelf, lawfully.** So the section cannot be right as written. The resolution is a distinction v2 collapses:

| Claim class | Chinese term | Available to 普通食品? |
|---|---|---|
| **Health-food function claim** (the 27 blue-hat functions — 减肥, 增强免疫力, 缓解体力疲劳 …) | 保健功能声称 | **No** — requires 蓝帽 registration |
| **Nutrient function claim** (dietary fibre, calcium, protein …) | 营养素功能声称 | **Yes**, when the content meets the threshold and the wording follows GB 28050 verbatim |

CHOCDAY's claim is a **GB 28050 营养素功能声称 for dietary fibre** — lawful on an ordinary food, not a blue-hat claim. That is why it can print it and why v2's "zero functional claims of any kind" cannot be literally true.

**So my PR #1839 §4 point survives, and gets sharper.** It is not a legal constraint; it is a **narrower** legal constraint than v2 states — the *standard-claim* register is open, the *blue-hat* register is closed. And the recommendation stands for the strategic reasons already given (CHOCDAY owns that ground; claims invite substantiation we don't need).

**Caveat on my own claim:** I am inferring the GB 28050 route from the printed pack and general practice, not from a regulatory opinion for this product. The direction is right — v2's "zero of any kind" is falsified by a live competitor — but the precise classification should be confirmed by a Chinese regulatory consultant before it goes into any label brief. What is *certain* is that v2's absolute statement is falsified on its own evidence.
