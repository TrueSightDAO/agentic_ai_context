## 0. Is the McCann framework relevant?

**Yes — and it falsifies part of my own prior positioning.**

McCann Worldgroup's guiding platform since 1912 is **"Truth Well Told"**: find an enduring *human* truth and tell it well. Their current leadership describes it as a growth framework rather than a creative philosophy, operationalised through a planning process called **Truth to Impact**.*

The relevant public statement of doctrine:

> "We unlock human truths and turn those truths into compelling stories… You move the market by moving people. And you're not going to move people by just telling them why **functional superiority** should command a price premium."

That line is a direct challenge to what I wrote in the field audit (§4). My positioning was:

> *"Agroverse is the single-origin Brazilian dark bar **whose farm of origin the buyer can look up**."*

Verifiability is a **functional superiority claim**. McCann's doctrine says that will not carry a price premium on its own — and the field evidence agrees: Meiji's 50% vs 70% with different single-origin claims earned **zero** price difference at identical pack size and price (annex §2.2). If a *better* claim earns nothing, *more verifiable* will not automatically earn ¥1,420/kg either.

**So this report restructures the positioning:** verifiability stays, but demoted to the **reason to believe**. The **reason to buy** moves onto a human truth. That is also what finally resolves the price problem, because a gift is not priced per kilogram.

\* *Truth Well Told is McCann's brand platform and Truth to Impact their planning process. What follows applies that human-truth-led logic; it is not a commissioned McCann engagement, and their proprietary process is not reproduced here.*

## 1. Two human truths, two different businesses

### 1.1 China — the truth is about the **giver**, not the eater

What the shelves actually showed:

| Evidence | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| **卡卡星座 / Zodiac Choco Cereal** — 30 g, Italian import, zodiac theme, **no cocoa % printed anywhere** | The concept *is* the product. Twelve-SKU collectible set built for gifting. |
| **Café-Tasse 77% — 20 g for ¥25** at blt | Premium is expressed as **small unit, high ¥/g** — an impulse-gift grammar, not a bar grammar. |
| Sam's gift formats: Ballotin ¥49.90, 音乐盒 ¥129.90, Rabitos ¥99.90 | Boxed gifting is a distinct, merchandised category. |
| blt wine: **cellars at 13.5 °C, bottles to ¥2,599** | This store already sells provenance and gift prices far above any chocolate on its shelves. |
| Kaiping chocolate-cluster policy: 侨乡手信 gift boxes, 工业旅游 | Industrial policy itself aims at **伴手礼** (courtesy gift). |

> **Human Truth (China):** *"When I give someone something, I want it to show how much thought went into choosing it — and I want to be able to prove I didn't just grab the first box off the shelf."*

Chinese gift culture runs on sincerity that can be **demonstrated** (用心, 心意). A QR code that opens onto a named farm, a named harvest and a real family is not a data feature — **it is the demonstrable form of 用心.** That is a human truth the pack can deliver, and no incumbent on those shelves can.

**Channel consequence:** the correct shelf analogue is **not the chocolate gondola — it is the wine/gift section.** A ¥71 bar competes against a ¥70–150 box of tea or a bottle of wine for the same gift occasion, not against ¥25 of Belgian chocolate on a ¥/kg basis. This resolves the pricing problem I flagged in annex §4.5: **¥1,420/kg was the wrong unit of comparison all along.**

In the USA cacao is an **ingredient in a functional routine** — spooned into coffee, cereal, yogurt, a smoothie. It is bought as a **bag**, judged on **carbs, protein, rawness, price**, and it must still *taste* good. It is not a gift and not a bar.

**The two markets are not the same business.** China is gifting and indulgence; the USA is a functional-ingredient market. One 274 kg lot cannot serve both with one SKU.

## 2. McCann "Truth Well Told" applied — China

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **Human Truth** | *When I give a gift, I want it to show how much thought went in — and I want to be able to prove it wasn't grabbed off a shelf.* |
| **Brand Truth** | Every bar traces to one named farm, one named harvest, and a public registry entry — which no imported brand on these shelves can show. |
| **The Idea** | **每一块，都说得清来历** — *Every bar can tell you exactly where it came from.* |
| **Reasons to Believe** | QR → DAO ledger per bar · named farm (La Do Sitio) · named harvest · 81% with real 可可脂 · single-origin 274 kg lot · 10,000-hectare restoration mission |
| **Impact** | Move the category axis from *"what percentage do you claim?"* to *"whose farm?"* — the one axis incumbents structurally cannot follow, because following it means building a registry, not printing a label. |

### 2.1 Why the idea survives the McCann test

- **Human**, not functional — it is about the giver's sincerity, not cocoa solids.
- **Provable** — the RTB is the registry, our actual asset.
- **Uncopyable short-run** — a competitor can print "single origin" tomorrow; they cannot produce per-bar lineage tomorrow.
- **Moves the price frame** out of ¥/kg and into the gift budget.

### 2.2 What this changes versus the prior brief

| Prior | Now |
|---|---|
| Positioning = "verifiable provenance" | Verifiable provenance is the **RTB**, not the positioning |
| Framed against chocolate shelf at ¥/kg | Framed against the **gift occasion** and the wine/gift section |
| ¥1,420/kg defended as "1.14× Café-Tasse" | ¥1,420/kg **withdrawn as the reference unit** — ¥59–71 is a gift price, not a per-kg price |
| "Fight on verifiability" | Fight on **sincerity**, evidenced by verifiability |

### 3.3 USA price reality

US average selling prices cluster **$13.94–$22.57**, commodities at $15–18. Our 50 g bar at $10 is **$200/kg** — roughly **3× the per-kg price of an 8 oz nib pouch at $15.84 ($69.80/kg)**. The USA bar lane is not where a $10/50 g product wins on Amazon. **The bag/ingredient lane is.**

| | **China** | **USA** |

| Channel | blt / Ole' / MixC-tier, gifting, D2C | Amazon keyword-led, D2C |
| Lead claim | *Every bar can tell you where it came from* | *A morning ritual you can trace* |
| Primary keyword / shelf | Farm lineage vs 98%/99% claims | ceremonial cacao, brewed cacao, cacao nibs |
| Competitive basis | Verifiable lineage | Price + carb/protein + rawness + taste |

**The 274 kg constraint:** La Do Sitio yields ≈391 kg chocolate ≈ **7,800 × 50 g bars** — enough for a China gift play, nowhere near an Amazon powder business. **Ceremonial cacao 200 g pouches stretch the same lot across both** (~1,950 pouches), the only allocation serving both truths.

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Lead with the human truth, not the ledger.** Gift sincerity in China; morning ritual in the USA. Verifiability is the proof, never the pitch. *(McCann doctrine and Meiji's zero-premium 50%-vs-70% result both point here.)*
2. **Re-frame ¥1,420/kg out of existence.** Price the China bar as a **gift at ¥59–71** against the wine/gift section, not per-kg against the chocolate gondola.
3. **Target blt / Ole' / MixC-tier for the gift SKU.** One novelty SKU does not defend the craft slot.
4. **Copy the collectible mechanic, not the zodiac** — one bar per named farm; the set is the collection.
5. **Cherry-pick USA on `ceremonial cacao`** — 149 competitors, $0.71 PPC, $19.53 ASP, and absent from every Chinese shelf. Bridge SKU.
6. **Enter the USA through the bag lane, not the bar lane** — nibs (42.29) and ceremonial cacao, not $200/kg bars on Amazon.
7. **Claim `cacao therapy` as a brand term in the USA** — 878 searches, zero competing products listed.
8. **Start the GACC application immediately.** 3–6 months, gates everything, not blocked on anything else.
9. **Do not fight on cocoa percentage.** Unwinnable against Beryl's 99% and CHOCDAY's 98%, and per McCann it would not carry a premium anyway.
10. **Two SKUs, two truths, one lot** — 50 g gift bars (China) + 200 g ceremonial pouches (both), allocating the 274 kg across them.
