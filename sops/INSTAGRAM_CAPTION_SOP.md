# SOP — Instagram caption writing (personal-IG vlog register)

> **Trigger:** a governor asks for "an Instagram caption", "caption for this reel", "what should I post with this".
> **Applies to:** `Gary Teh` posting reels to his **personal Instagram**.
> **Last verified:** 2026-11-24.

---

## 1. The channel reality (read this first)

One caption, **three surfaces**:

| Surface | How it gets there |
|---|---|
| **Personal Instagram** (`@gary`) | the original post |
| **Agroverse Instagram** | **automated cross-post** |
| **Personal Facebook** | **automated cross-post** |

Consequences that change the writing:

- **Write for the personal feed first.** The primary reader is someone who follows *Gary*, not a brand. They are not a customer yet — they are a witness.
- **Do not write brand-voice copy.** Marketing-register captions ("Introducing…", "We're proud to announce…", "Discover the difference…") read as an ad on a personal feed and get scrolled. On the Agroverse account the same words look merely bland; on the personal feed they look **inauthentic**, which is worse.
- **The viewer's mental model is a vlog.** They are watching a person learn a craft and build something. The caption's job is to be that person's voice, not the company's.
- **Cross-posting is automatic — never add platform-specific instructions** ("link in bio", "swipe up", "comment below and I'll DM you") that don't hold on all three surfaces.

---

## 2. Register

- **First person, present tense.** "I keep thinking about…", "What I'm learning is…".
- **The governor is the narrator.** Never write as "Agroverse" or "we the company" unless quoting a decision.
- **Admitting limits is the point, not a weakness.** A vlog's credibility comes from the narrator not knowing everything. "Still learning this machine" outperforms manufactured authority.
- **Warm, specific, unhurried.** No hype, no exclamation stacking, no emoji rows.

---

## 3. Structure (six parts, in order)

| # | Part | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | First ~**125 characters** must carry the post alone — everything after is behind "more". One sentence. A tension or an observation, not a summary. |
| 2 | **The problem** | The concrete difficulty in the footage. Physical, not abstract. |
| 3 | **What the footage actually shows** | Describe the real thing — verified against the frames, not against the beat's intended meaning. |
| 4 | **What he is learning** | One honest reflection. The vlog payload. |
| 5 | **Mission tie-back** | **Once.** Concrete link to restoring 10,000 hectares of Amazon rainforest (`PURPOSE_AND_MISSION.md`). |
| 6 | **Question** | A real question that invites the tribe to answer. One only. |

Blank line between parts. Short paragraphs — 1–3 sentences.

---

## 4. Hard constraints

1. **≤ 2,200 characters** (Instagram cap).
2. **The caption must not contradict the on-screen text.** Read the reel's actual `COPY` block first; the caption is a companion, not a correction.
3. **Never assert unverified personal history or logistics.** Do not invent "the first time I…", travel claims, or a supply-chain route the governor has not confirmed. If a line depends on such a fact, **flag it to the governor** for confirmation rather than shipping it silently. A route printed on screen (e.g. an origin × roast-city overlay) is a rendering claim, not verified logistics.
4. **Mission stated once, concretely.** "10,000 hectares of Amazon rainforest" — never "save the planet", never as a hashtag slogan.
5. **Hashtags: 3–6, relevant, lowercase.** The canonical source is the `Hashtag suggestions` tab of the content sheet (`CONTENT_MARKETING_SCHEDULE.md`). Never a wall of 30.
6. **No claims about product effects** (health, medicinal, ceremonial benefits) unless the governor has approved that language.

---

## 5. Anti-patterns (each one has burned us)

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Brand-voice opener ("Introducing our new…") | Reads as an ad on a personal feed |
| Three CTAs | Permission marketing is one ask, not a funnel |
| "Link in bio" | Breaks the Facebook cross-post; also spammy |
| Restating the mission as a slogan | Turns a goal into decoration |
| Over-claiming ("we are saving the rainforest") | Unsupportable; invites the exact scepticism the project can't afford |
| Describing the *intended* beat, not the *actual* frame | Ships a false claim in the caption above a false claim on screen |
| Emoji as punctuation | Drowns the narrator's voice |
| Hashtag wall | Signals reach-buying |

---

## 6. QC-honesty rule (non-negotiable)

If the reel's on-screen text is **known to be inaccurate** — established by frame verification during the QC gate (`MEDIA_ARCHIVE_PIPELINE.md`) — then:

1. The caption must be written so it **does not repeat or reinforce** the bad claim.
2. The defect must be **disclosed to the governor** alongside the caption, with the specific beat and what the frame actually shows.
3. Never let good copy launder a bad frame. The caption passing is not evidence the reel passed.

---

## 7. Checklist before returning a caption

- [ ] Read the reel's actual `COPY` block / on-screen text
- [ ] Read the verified frame descriptions from the QC gate, if they exist
- [ ] Identify every factual claim and mark which ones need governor confirmation
- [ ] Hook ≤ 125 chars, carries alone
- [ ] Exactly one mission tie-back, concrete
- [ ] Exactly one question
- [ ] ≤ 2,200 chars, ≤ 6 hashtags
- [ ] No platform-specific instruction that breaks a cross-post
- [ ] Any on-screen inaccuracy disclosed to the governor

---

## 8. Deliverable format

Return, in this order:

1. **The caption** (copy-paste ready, no surrounding quotes)
2. **Hashtag block** on its own line
3. **A short alternative** (punchier, ~40% of the length) for when the primary feels long
4. **Claims to confirm** — a bulleted list of anything asserted about the governor's personal experience or logistics
5. **On-screen inaccuracies**, if any (per §6) — with beat, claimed text, and what the frame actually shows
